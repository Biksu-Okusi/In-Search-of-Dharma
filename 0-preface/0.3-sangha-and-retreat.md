# 0.3 The Sangha & the Retreat

> ◉ **Public edition.** This note is published in a redacted public edition. Personal contact details, private third-party specifics, and passages resting on the author's private records are excluded, and each exclusion is disclosed where it is made. The private sources themselves (correspondence, family records, the mail archive) are held by the author and are not part of the public repository.

**Question:** Document the lived community and the formative encounter: the author's small sangha (himself, Rupert Bozeat, Elfie), their first meeting on 13 February 2018 at the first of six five-day Bodhi College retreats on Secular Dharma in West Sussex taught by Stephen Batchelor; the influence of Batchelor's "After Buddhism"; and the "a secular dharma?" YouTube channel (~50 recorded conversations, transcripts folded into the knowledgebases).

## Synthesis

The author's "tiny sangha" is a three-person discussion group — Gary Dean, Rupert Bozeat and Elfie Klinger — that formed out of a Bodhi College secular-dharma course taught by Stephen Batchelor and continued as an ongoing online conversation [S1][S15]. Batchelor's *After Buddhism: Rethinking the Dharma for a Secular Age* (Yale UP, 2015) — which reframes the dharma as four *tasks* to be done rather than four truths to be believed — is the book the author names as the draw to the course, and its "Ten Theses of Secular Dharma" became the group's shared text [S8][S17][S21][S22]. The group recorded roughly fifty of its conversations and posted them to a public YouTube channel, "a secular dharma?" (@aseculardharma), whose participants describe themselves as graduates of that course; the cleaned transcripts were then folded into the very `appliedanthropology`/`seculardharma` corpus this essay series queries [S10][S25][S28]. Two attitudes run through the record and matter for the series: a deliberate *ambivalence about the label "Buddhism"* (shared by the sangha and by Batchelor himself, who says he would eventually drop the words *Buddhist*, *Buddhism*, "and maybe even the word Dharma") and a matching *distrust of any single site "speaking for" secular dharma* — the reason the author let his own seculardharma.net domain lapse [S11][S12][S15]. The encounter's dated spine is firm: the two-year Secular Dharma course began on **13 February 2018** and ran as **six five-day intensive retreats** — one in England (the West Sussex leg the author recalls), one in the Netherlands and four in Belgium — woven together with many online meetings of the whole group and smaller subgroups, and bracketed by other online courses the author took under Batchelor before and after [S15][S23][S24]. (A separate, later Batchelor online course, "After Buddhism and Beyond," also opened on a 13 February — in 2021 — which is simply why that date recurs in the public record, not a rival reading of the 2018 start [S29][S31].)

## Findings

### The sangha: who they are and how they formed

The author's own account (the autobiographical SEED, a local primary source) names "my tiny sangha … comprises myself Elfie, and Rupert" and dates its origin: "Rupert, Elfie, and I met on the 13th of February, 2018 … over 3000 days ago. it was in West Sussex, at the first of six 5-day retreats run by Bodhi College … on the topic of Secular Dharma. the main teacher of this course was Stephen Batchelor, whose book 'After Buddhism' was influential in me taking this course" [S15]; he glosses the group warmly as "my little coterie — sangha, i suppose" [S16].

The corpus corroborates this independently of the SEED. In a message to the wider list, **Rupert Bozeat** writes: "Gary, Elfie and I have … continued our small tutor group discussions online. Somewhere along the line it was decided to record them and — in a small effort to promote the concept of secular dharma — post them on youtube" [S1]. Elfie's surname is confirmed as **Klinger** by a sangha discussion-email recipient list (address not reproduced) [S2]; Rupert's surname **Bozeat** by his being the named sender/administrator of the seculardharma.net list messages [S3][S6]. Both namings are already public: the book's own Preface and the channel's public record carry them with the members' knowledge. The group thus began as a *tutor group* within the course and outlived it as a standing conversation [S1][S4]. Notably, the impulse behind this *whole "defining dharma" enterprise* is documented as originating inside a sangha meeting: the recorded conversations "were initially stimulated by Gary's show stopping presentation at our last meeting … when he suggested there should be a definition of dharma," branching into "what is dharma, how is it useful and … how do we engage more people with it?" [S1]. The DD project's own seed sits in this community.

### Bodhi College, the course, and Stephen Batchelor

**Stephen Batchelor** (b. 7 April 1953, Dundee) is "a noted proponent of agnostic or secular Buddhism," who trained first as a Tibetan (Gelug) monk from 1974 and then as a Sŏn (Korean Zen) monk at Songgwangsa under Kusan Sunim (1981–84) before laicising and marrying Martine Fages in 1985 [S17][S18]. He co-founded Sharpham College (Devon, 1996) and is a co-founder of **Bodhi College**, "an educational organization dedicated to exploring the dharma as found in the earliest Buddhist texts through courses combining study and retreats" [S17][S19][S23]. The corpus documents the same founding — Batchelor with Christina Feldman, John Peacock and a former Theravada monk — and the College's remit as "early Buddhism," the Pali Canon discourses "before … any particular Buddhist school or orthodoxy" hardened around them [S7].

The course the SEED describes is Bodhi College's **two-year modular "Secular Dharma" programme**, taught by **Stephen Batchelor, Martine Batchelor, Renate Seifarth and Jennie Wilks**, combining study, practice and discussion over two years from 13 February 2018 [S23][S27]. By the author's own account it ran as **six five-day residential retreats** — one in England (the West Sussex leg), one in the Netherlands and four in Belgium — alongside many online meetings of the full group and smaller subgroups; he also took other online Bodhi College / Batchelor courses both before and after it [S15]. (One of those later online offerings, "After Buddhism and Beyond," ran 13 Feb–26 June 2021 over Zoom — a *distinct* course from the 2018 in-person cohort, and the reason the same calendar date appears in the public record [S29][S30][S31].) A first-hand reflection by **Stefano Bettera** (Secular Buddhist Network, 4 Dec 2019) records the **2018–2019 cohort** specifically — "taught by Martine Batchelor, Stephen Batchelor, Jennie Wilks, and Renate Seifarth," with "a group of twenty people" completing it — the cohort the SEED is describing (Batchelor as "main teacher," beginning early 2018) [S24]. Bettera is the *same* correspondent named in the corpus's 2018–19 seculardharma.net "Ten Theses" list threads (see [S6], [S14]) — a tidy external/KB cross-confirmation that this circle existed and overlapped [S24]. In one of the sangha's own recorded talks (a folded-in transcript) the speaker addressed as Gary and the group reflect that they were on the **second** secular-dharma cohort, "After Buddhism and Beyond," note Elfie's explanatory role, and judge that "Bodhi College has an ethos … committed to Buddhism" while "what we and others are looking for is something different" — adding that "Stephen … said at the end that he doesn't want to use the word Buddhism anymore … he uses Dharma instead" [S4][S5].

### *After Buddhism* and the "Ten Theses"

The book the SEED names is **Stephen Batchelor, *After Buddhism: Rethinking the Dharma for a Secular Age* (New Haven: Yale University Press, 2015; ISBN 9780300224344)** — "the culmination of four decades of study and practice in the Tibetan, Zen, and Theravada traditions," depicting the Buddha "as a pragmatic ethicist rather than a dogmatic metaphysician" and his dharma as "task-based ethics rather than truth-based metaphysics" [S20][S21]. Batchelor's own framing: it advances "Ten Theses of Secular Dharma" and reframes the dharma as four *tasks* — embrace suffering, let go of reactivity, behold its ceasing, cultivate an integrated way of life — a "logic of care" replacing a "logic of transcendence" [S8][S9]. The corpus holds the full Ten Theses (dated 2018), including Thesis 7's framing of community as "autonomous persons who mutually support each other" rather than a hierarchy [S8]. *After Buddhism* sits in a wider Batchelor corpus the knowledgebase references — *Buddhism Without Beliefs* (1997, "the watershed" that "laid the groundwork for the secular Buddhist movement"), *Confession of a Buddhist Atheist* (2010), *Secular Buddhism* (2017), *The Art of Solitude* (2020), and, with Martine, *What Is This?* (2019) [S17][S22].

### The "a secular dharma?" YouTube channel

The channel the author says "has mattered far more to me, and to my little coterie" is **"a secular dharma?"** at handle **@aseculardharma** (stable URL youtube.com/channel/UCJ9zuxSNKALELgjGBMtOC_w) [S15][S25]. In his words: "that's mostly myself, rupert and elfie talking things through. we made around 50 videos … transcripts of these talks have been cleaned up and folded into the secular dharma / applied anthropology knowledgebases," and he flags one video (youtube.com/watch?v=dZ5GzrCpHQ0) as sketching what he has done with that data [S15]. The channel's public "About" text confirms the account: its creators are "graduates of the Bodhi College two-year Secular Dharma course run by Stephen Batchelor," posting "their own musings and interpretations on concepts introduced in that course, alongside sources from philosophy, art, science, information technology …" [S25][S28]. Public videos include a numbered series — e.g. "17 Creating a taxonomy of dharma: are aphorisms useful?" (`foBEsT0UiMo`) and "A Brief History of Secular Dharma" (`XHY9st-NUII`) — consistent with "around 50 videos," though the exact count is the author's own figure and is not independently verifiable from the listing [S28].

The corpus *is the result of that folding-in*: the sangha-talk transcripts appear in it as documents named with YouTube-style eleven-character video IDs (`8S9p2Qv7tHw.transcript.txt`, `EbvKgvH9Occ.transcript.txt`, `gVGiyHdUxf0.transcript.txt`, `5BMfMJe7K8g.transcript.txt`, among others), confirming that the recorded conversations were transcribed and ingested [S4][S5][S10]. The Secular Buddhist Network independently documents the channel and sangha by name: a Feb 2024 article cites "Gary Dean … from the 'a secular dharma?' YouTube channel," names "Elfie" and "Rupert" as participants in a discussion titled "Crafting Dharmic AI," and describes Gary's project to "teach AI about secular dharma and human behavioral evolution … by transcribing videos, creating coherent paragraphs, and developing models to sit alongside other large language models (LLMs) to influence their values" — independent public corroboration of the sangha membership, the channel, and the knowledgebase project that *is* this series' substrate [S26].

### Ambivalence about the label "Buddhism"

A deliberate distance from the word "Buddhism" runs through the record. The author states it of himself — "i have a similar allergy to 'buddhism'" — said of Batchelor being "temperamentally allergic to labels more than the content, with good reason," an allergy he shares [S15]. The sangha states it collectively in Rupert Bozeat's on-camera channel intro: "We are not particularly interested in Buddhism and see it as a system that took innovative philosophical ideas, which we now refer to as dharma, and constructed a religion around them," while remaining "very grateful that those ideas have been preserved by Buddhism" [S10]. Batchelor states it in his own voice in the corpus: "I don't like the word Buddhism particularly … At a certain point … we're going to have to drop the words Buddhist, Buddhism, and maybe even the word Dharma," and elsewhere that "a secular dharma may very well end up decoupling itself from traditional Buddhism and perhaps dropping the word Buddhist altogether" [S11][S12]. The sangha read this very tension into Bodhi College, judging it — "set up to study early Buddhism" — "very limited … as a base for developing an expression of genuinely secular dharma" [S4]. The essay series inherits this ambivalence: gratitude to the tradition that preserved the ideas, coupled with reluctance to keep wearing its name.

### Ambivalence about any single site "speaking for" secular dharma

A matching distrust of institutional centralisation runs alongside it. The author: "i first put up seculardharma.net many years ago, at Stephen's request, actually. For a range of reasons it never really took off — and, to be honest, i was never completely sold on the concept of a single site speaking for 'secular dharma' in the first place. i let the domain lapse just a few months back" [S15]. The stance has an institutional parallel: the Secular Buddhist movement has "resisted promoting a set of settled answers," declining to create "a new set of doctrines, with their potential to ossify, first into dogmas and then into institutions and hierarchies," preferring "an open conversation" [S13] — echoed in the Ten Theses' framing of community as autonomous mutual support rather than hierarchy [S8]. That seculardharma.net was a live list/forum is corroborated in the corpus: the mailing list debated Batchelor's "Ten Theses" in 2018–19, with Rupert Bozeat and other correspondents (among them Stefano Davide Bettera, who has written publicly of this circle) posting [S6]. Bettera captures the same ambivalence: "Secular Dharma or Buddhism? … A label in fact … the door opens to the emergence of new doctrinal orthodoxies of which I personally feel little need" [S14]. The distrust of a single authoritative site is thus not idiosyncratic but a recognised current within the wider conversation.

## Tensions, gaps & corpus bias

- **The dated spine, clarified by the author and consistent with the public record.** The course began **13 February 2018** and comprised **six five-day intensive retreats** — England ×1 (the West Sussex leg), the Netherlands ×1 and Belgium ×4 — plus many online meetings of the full group and subgroups; the author also took other online Batchelor courses before and after [S15]. External sources independently confirm the 2018–19 Bodhi College two-year secular-dharma programme taught by Batchelor (Bettera's first-hand account of the ~20-person cohort) [S24][S23][S27]. The one apparent clash the initial research pass raised — that "13 February" is *publicly* documented for a 2021 course — dissolves once the two are separated: the online "After Buddhism and Beyond" (13 Feb–26 June 2021, Zoom) is one of those *other, later* courses, not the 2018 cohort, so the date simply recurs [S29][S30][S31]. What still rests on the author's own account alone is the country-by-country retreat breakdown and the "six" count [S15]; the start date, the teacher, and the two-year cohort are externally corroborated.

- **"~50 videos" and "transcripts folded into the KBs" rest partly on self-report.** The corpus corroborates the *folding-in* directly — the transcripts are present as video-ID-named documents [S4][S5][S10] — and the public channel and a third-party article corroborate the channel's existence and the sangha's identity [S26][S28]. But the exact "around 50" count is the author's own figure [S15]; the public listing does not confirm it.

- **The sangha's own talks are corpus material — self-referential by construction.** Several of the strongest corroborating sources ([S1], [S4], [S5], [S10]) are the sangha's own recorded conversations and list messages, now *inside* the corpus being researched: genuine primary evidence of what the group said, but not *independent* of the subject — the people corroborating the sangha are the sangha. Honest provenance for a Part-0 note, not a defect; but a Stage-2 essay should not present the group's self-description as external attestation. The genuinely third-party corroborations are Bettera's SBN reflection [S24], the SBN AI article naming all three members [S26], the public channel "About" text [S25][S28], and the Batchelor/Bodhi College biography and bibliography [S17][S19][S20].

- **Secular / Western-academic lean of the corpus shaped the framing.** The shared corpus is curated around the author's own influences — Batchelor prominent among them, alongside Boehm, Sapolsky, Graeber and Bregman — so its account of *After Buddhism* and the Ten Theses is sympathetic and internal to the secular-Buddhist conversation, not critical of it. There is little from traditional Theravada or Tibetan voices who would contest the "after Buddhism" move or defend the metaphysics Batchelor sets aside; the label-ambivalence is presented from inside the camp that holds it. The external sources (Wikipedia, Yale UP, SBN) situate Batchelor but are themselves broadly within the secular/agnostic-Buddhist orbit. A Stage-2 balancing pass would want a traditionalist critique, which neither the corpus nor this note supplies.

- **Bereavements and political history are author's-words-only, deliberately.** Where this note touches the author's life beyond the sangha — the bereavements bracketing his 2014 intensification (his son, 2012; wife Anita, 2015) and his earlier political arc (CPA membership, then the Freedom Collective) — those are recorded solely from his own account [S15], not web-searched or corroborated, per the project's privacy policy on private family and political history. Noted here only as context around the sangha years; the substantive treatment belongs to [[0.1-life-and-formation]].

- **Private correspondence cited for substance only.** The Dean–Margolis email thread is treated as a local primary source for its *substance* — the people, the channel, the books and ideas exchanged, the "single site" ambivalence, the "allergy to 'buddhism'." All personal contact details (phone, postal and personal email addresses) were stripped and are never reproduced [S16].
