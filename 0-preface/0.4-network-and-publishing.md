# 0.4 Network & Publishing

> ◉ **Public edition.** This note is published in a redacted public edition. It rests substantially on a private correspondence, and the correspondent has reviewed the material and consented to its publication (August 2026). Personal contact details are excluded throughout; passages resting on the author's private records are excluded, with each exclusion disclosed where it is made. The private sources themselves (the correspondence, the mail archive) are held by the author and are not part of the public repository.

**Question:** Map the wider secular-dharma network the author is embedded in: Ramsey Margolis and the Tuwhiri Project (NZ publisher; the "Buddhism In A Nutshell" series; Margolis' forthcoming "Creativity and secular dharma" linking Batchelor to Graeber/Ward/Prichard); Paul Regan; Winton Higgins and "After Buddhism: a workbook" (2018 Kickstarter); "Mindful Solidarity"; the Batchelors' "What is This? Ancient questions for modern minds" and its January 2026 Ezra Klein interview; and the lapsed seculardharma.net / secularbuddhism.net history.

## Synthesis

The author is one node in a loose, transnational secular-dharma network with several centres of gravity — Bodhi College (the teaching venue where he and his sangha formed), the Secular Buddhist Network, the author's own sangha, and, as its **publishing** node, the **Tuwhiri Project**, a small Aotearoa-New-Zealand / multinational secular-Buddhist publisher founded in August 2018 and run by publisher **Ramsey Margolis** [S10][S11]. (The fuller *human* network behind the essays — teachers, sangha, cohort and correspondents — is set down in [[0.7-influences-and-acknowledgments]]; this note maps the **publishing** strand and the texts that travel along it, in which Margolis is one node, not the hub.) The network's defining texts — Higgins' *After Buddhism: a workbook* (2018, the Kickstarter the author backed), Slott's *Mindful Solidarity* (2024), and the Batchelors' *What is This?* (2019) — are all Tuwhiri titles, and all three are physically present in the author's `appliedanthropology`/`seculardharma` corpus, which is the verifiable trace that the relationships the private correspondence describes are real [S1][S3][S4][S10][S12][S13][S14]. The connective thesis the author and Margolis share — that **dharma and mutual aid are two vocabularies for the same evolved cooperative machinery**, bridging Stephen Batchelor's secular dharma to the anarchist anthropology of David Graeber (and, in Margolis' planned book, Colin Ward and Alex Prichard) — is endorsed by the corpus, but that is self-referential corroboration: the corpus is the author's own curation and the texts are all from inside the movement [S2][S4][S5][S15]. The author's *own* contribution to the network is the non-institutional "a secular dharma?" YouTube sangha (Gary, Rupert Bozeat, Elfie), ~50 recorded chats whose cleaned-up transcripts he folded into the knowledgebases, plus a now-lapsed `seculardharma.net` domain he had originally registered at Batchelor's request and let go [S1][S6][S7][S16]. Crucially, the *relationships and editorial plans* (Margolis, Paul Regan, the forthcoming *Creativity and secular dharma*) are known almost entirely from one private email thread between two interested parties; no skeptical or outside-the-movement source on this network was found in either the corpus or the public web, and that absence is named here rather than smoothed over [S5][S15].

## Findings

### The Tuwhiri Project — the publishing node

The network's **publishing** node is **The Tuwhiri Project**, a secular-Buddhist publisher that describes its remit as books on "early Buddhism, its retrieval, and a secular adaptation to 21st-century conditions," foregrounding the "ethic of care" in Gotama's teaching [S11]. **Ramsey Margolis** is named as its publisher [S11]. The imprint was founded in **August 2018**, growing out of secular-Buddhist practitioners in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand, and operates across Wellington, Sydney, Chicago and the UK Midlands through two legal entities — **The Tuwhiri Project Ltd** (Aotearoa NZ) and **Tuwhiri US LLC** (New Jersey) [S11]. The name "Tuwhiri" is *te reo Māori* for revealing or making known — "discovering something lost or hidden" — chosen to express what its founders stood for as secular Buddhist practitioners [S11]. Margolis dates his own secular-Buddhism interest to **2005**, when he read Batchelor's theses on secular Buddhism, and first ran a secular-Buddhism course in Wellington in 2007 [S12].

Margolis' route into this work is itself a small instance of the recurring pattern the author flags (see below): in the private correspondence Margolis recounts buying two books both titled *An Introduction to Social Anthropology* around 1969 as a London teenager, failing to win a university place to study the subject, "stumbl[ing] into the printing industry" to become a typesetter, and seeing his early anthropological interest "morph into an interest in anarchist politics" — an arc he had thought "left behind some forty years ago" until a friend gave him Graeber's *Fragments of an Anarchist Anthropology* [S5].

### Paul Regan, and what is email-only

**Paul Regan** appears in the correspondence as a Tuwhiri colleague (cc'd on the thread) with whom Margolis "discovered" a shared long-standing interest in anthropology "when we first met, earlier this year" [S5]. Regan does **not** appear in any public source on this network that the external pass could find (searched against Bodhi College / Batchelor / secular dharma); his role is therefore known *only* from the private correspondence and is flagged as such [S15].

### "Buddhism In A Nutshell" and the forthcoming *Creativity and secular dharma*

Tuwhiri's forthcoming **"Buddhism In A Nutshell"** series is a set of short, popular, non-academic introductions — each 10,000–15,000 words, pocket-sized, written "for general readers rather than academics" with minimal footnotes, and most titled "… and secular dharma" — set to launch in **Q2 2026** with three titles: *Addiction, freedom and secular dharma* (Cathryn Jacob & Vince Cullen); *Ethics and secular dharma* (Carmel Shalev); and *Feelings, flourishing and secular dharma* (Martine Batchelor) [S17]. Further planned topics include jazz, forgiveness, karma/rebirth, relationships, work and identity [S17].

Margolis told the author he is "pulling together a text for the Buddhism In A Nutshell series with the working title of *Creativity and secular dharma*, in which I intend to bring together Batchelor's secular dharma with the kind of anarchism professed by people such as Graeber, Colin Ward, and Alex Prichard," and asked the author to suggest useful texts [S5]. The public record *partly* supports this: Margolis runs a **"Creative Dharma"** Substack newsletter under the Tuwhiri banner (co-produced with Brad Parks) on "how we might use creativity in our meditation practice, and bring meditative sensibility into our artistic practice," framed as a creative response to "climate emergency, social inequality and exclusion, species extinction" [S18][S19]. But the public sources attest only the *theme* (creativity + secular dharma); they do **not** mention a forthcoming book titled *Creativity and secular dharma*, nor the specific Batchelor → Graeber/Ward/Prichard linkage — that title-and-thesis claim is email-only and is cited as such [S5][S18][S19].

### Winton Higgins, *After Buddhism: a workbook*, and the 2018 Kickstarter

The first network text with a direct personal tie to the author is **Winton Higgins'** *After Buddhism: a workbook* — a study guide to Batchelor's *After Buddhism*, authored by Higgins "with Jim Champion and Ramsey Margolis," published by Tuwhiri in 2018 (ISBN 978-0473445171), and crowd-funded by a Kickstarter that ended **11 July 2018** and "brought in far more than expected," pre-selling copies worldwide [S20][S21]. Margolis' very first line to the author records that almost the only thing he knew of him was "that you supported the 2018 Kickstarter campaign for Winton Higgins's book *After Buddhism: a workbook*" [S5]. The corpus confirms the text is folded in: it is present as `After_Buddhism-a_workbook.md` (segments :3, :69), carrying study-guide commentary on Batchelor (e.g. :69 on doubt and "scepticism in the western tradition") [S3]. Tuwhiri itself, on Higgins' account, grew partly out of Higgins emailing his Sydney dharma-talk scripts to Margolis in Wellington [S11].

### *Mindful Solidarity* — the engaged-Buddhism / mutual-aid text

The text that occasioned the 2026 correspondence (its subject line is "New message about Mindful Solidarity") is **Mike Slott's** *Mindful Solidarity: A Secular Buddhist Democratic Socialist Dialogue* (Tuwhiri, 9 October 2024) [S5][S22]. Slott is a lifelong US labour-movement activist and **editor of the Secular Buddhist Network** website; he wrote the book at the suggestion of Tuwhiri's editorial board, joining "a secular approach to the teachings of Gotama" with "democratic socialist politics" and an "ethic of care," arguing that individual and social transformation must be pursued together [S22][S23]. The corpus holds it with substance as `Mindful-Solidarity.md` (segments :0, :11, :31, :94, :175): it is explicitly "a secular, radically engaged approach to Buddhism," addressed jointly "To Buddhists" and "To Left activists," arguing that meditation and ethics cannot be fully developed "in a society which harms people and reinforces greed and hatred," that capitalism must be "challenged by powerful mass movements, including a revived labor movement," and that Buddhist "skillful action" should be broadened from the individual to "community organizing struggles" and to subverting "the hegemony of the capitalist class — a key focus of the Italian Marxist, Antonio Gramsci" [S4]. This text is the clearest corpus-level statement of the dharma-as-mutual-aid bridge that the author calls "precisely my thesis" [S2][S4].

### The Batchelors' *What is This?* and the January 2026 Ezra Klein interview

**Martine & Stephen Batchelor's** *What is This? Ancient questions for modern minds* (Tuwhiri, 2019; ISBN 978-0473474973) was drawn from talks at a **Sŏn (Korean Zen)** retreat in England in 2016 and teaches the radical-questioning practice "What is this?", melding it with natural science, Western philosophy, Romantic poetry and early Buddhism [S24]. Margolis sent the author an ePub of it as "an apology for the book's earlier non-arrival" [S5]. The corpus confirms it as `Martine+Stephen-Batchelor_What-is-this.md` (segments :0, :21, :72, :94): :0 carries Pat Enkyo O'Hara's cover endorsement describing its origin in "Sŏn – Korean Zen – teachings," and :21 has Stephen on the Socratic "wonder is the beginning of philosophy" and the *kongan/kōan* "Why is there anything at all?" [S13].

The text gained sudden reach through an **Ezra Klein × Stephen Batchelor** interview released on *The Ezra Klein Show* (New York Times) on **2 January 2026**, titled **"This Question Can Change Your Life"** (65 min), covering the Seon "What is this?" practice and Batchelor's reframing of the Four Noble Truths as four tasks, and referencing Batchelor's latest book *Buddha, Socrates, and Us: Ethical Living in Uncertain Times* [S25][S26]. The YouTube posting of that same interview — "The Most Powerful Question I've Heard | The Ezra Klein Show" — is the link Margolis sent and the link in the question's brief; it is *not* a sangha video [S26][S27]. The author wrote that he made "a cleaned up transcript … of that Klein interview you sent (no, I had not seen it before)," and the corpus confirms this exactly: it holds `The-Most-Powerful-Question-Ive-Heard.md:0`, whose opening is Klein's own year-end framing naming *What Is This?* — i.e. the author's own cleaned-up transcript of the January 2026 episode [S1][S14]. One unusually independent, non-private corroboration anchors the interview's impact: on the Secular Buddhist Network, Margolis is quoted *publicly* that over **700 copies** of *What is This?* were bought within a week of the transcript's publication [S26].

### The author's own sangha and the lapsed domains

The author's personal contribution to the network is deliberately *non-institutional*. In the correspondence he writes that "what has mattered far more to me, and to my little coterie — sangha, I suppose — is the 'a secular dharma?' YouTube channel … that's mostly myself, Rupert and Elfie talking things through. We made around 50 videos … transcripts of these talks have been cleaned up and folded into the secular dharma / applied anthropology knowledgebases" [S1]. The channel — **@aseculardharma** (channel ID UCJ9zuxSNKALELgjGBMtOC_w) — resolves as a live channel; its creators are described publicly as graduates of **Bodhi College's** two-year Secular Dharma course run by Stephen Batchelor, and **Rupert Bozeat** is named publicly as a contributor [S16][S28]. The corpus confirms the talks were folded in: `gVGiyHdUxf0.transcript.txt:0` opens "Hi, my name is Rupert Bozeat. Some friends and I are involved in discussions around concepts related to secular dharma. We meet up once a week or so … We are not particularly interested in Buddhism and see it as a system that took innovative philosophical ideas, which we now refer to as dharma, and constructed a religion around them" — the author's "Rupert" introducing the very chats the email describes [S6]. A large set of cleaned-up sangha notes and raw transcripts is present (`a-secular-dharma_2025-07-26.md`, `a-secular-dharma_ai-alignment`, `secular-dharma-discussions.txt`, and many YouTube-style transcript files) [S7]. The Bodhi College course is publicly confirmed as a two-year modular programme taught by Stephen Batchelor with Martine Batchelor, Renate Seifarth and Jenny Wilks, centred on reconfiguring the Four Noble Truths as Four Tasks — the course the author's own account dates to a first West Sussex retreat on 13 February 2018 [S29][S1].

On the lapsed domains, the author's own account is the primary source: "yes, I first put up `seculardharma.net` many years ago, at Stephen's request, actually. For a range of reasons it never really took off — and … I was never completely sold on the concept of a single site speaking for 'secular dharma' in the first place. I let the domain lapse just a few months back, and it seems to have already found a new home" [S1]. Margolis, in the same thread, separately refers to the author's former coterie "around a website at `secularbuddhism.net`" — the two domain names appear to have been used loosely/interchangeably in the correspondence for the same lapsed presence [S5]. Reported neutrally, as instructed: as of June 2026 a direct fetch of `seculardharma.net` failed with a TLS certificate name-mismatch (`ERR_TLS_CERT_ALTNAME_INVALID`) — the signature of a domain no longer serving its former site — and `secularbuddhism.net` returned no retrievable content (request timed out) [S30][S31]. Neither is the author's site, and `secularbuddhism.net` is *distinct* from the active, unrelated **Secular Buddhist Association** (`secularbuddhism.org`) and **Secular Buddhist Network** (`secularbuddhistnetwork.org`) [S31]. The adjacent "secular dharma" namespace is now occupied by several unrelated parties — e.g. a Secular Dharma Foundation and Margolis' own Creative Dharma Substack — but no claim is made about what `seculardharma.net` "is now" beyond: no longer the author's site, the name sitting in a namespace used by unrelated parties [S30][S31].

### The thesis that knits the network together

The intellectual through-line, in the author's own words, is that "you already felt the closeness between Graeber and Batchelor when you read *Fragments*; for what it's worth, that closeness is precisely my thesis. Dharmas and mutual aid are both, in the end, evolved ways for a particular cooperative primate to live well together. Different vocabularies but the same underlying machinery" [S2]. He grounds it in human behavioural biology — naming David Sloan Wilson's *This View of Life* (and Prosocial, "its practical offshoot"), Christopher Boehm's *Hierarchy in the Forest* "on how foragers actively hold their egalitarianism in place," and Sarah Hrdy's *Mothers and Others* "on where our cooperative instincts come from" — read alongside Kropotkin, so that "the whole 'anarchist anthropology' framing stops looking romantic and starts looking like plain natural history" [S2]. He explicitly frames the relationship between his two knowledgebases the same way: "applied anthropology is the substrate; secular dharma is one of the applications running on top of it," so "Graeber and Sapolsky sit quite naturally alongside Batchelor — different vocabularies, but the same underlying project" [S2]. The corpus that the network's texts have been folded into is, by the author's account, "over 14,000 curated books, articles and transcripts" with a "cast" of Graeber, Sapolsky, Boehm, Batchelor, Wrangham, Bregman, James Scott, Sarah Hrdy and Lisa Feldman Barrett — and it explicitly includes Margolis' own "writings and talks" [S2]. The author also notes a recurring social pattern: "anthropology into anarchist politics, I keep noticing the same constellation in a subset of people drawn into recent secular dharma communities," and reads Batchelor's own reluctance to accept the "green anarchism" label as temperamental — "allergic to labels more than the content … I have a similar allergy to 'buddhism'" [S2].

## Tensions, gaps & corpus bias

- **The corpus's confirmation is self-referential, not independent.** The KB's job here is *provenance*: it reliably shows that the network's texts (*What is This?*, the Klein transcript, *After Buddhism: a workbook*, *Mindful Solidarity*, the sangha talks) physically reside in the author's corpus, which is the traceable evidence that the email's relationships are real [S3][S4][S6][S13][S14]. But the corpus is the author's *own* curation, heavily Batchelor / secular-Buddhist-weighted (*After Buddhism*, the Ten Theses, *Secular Buddhism*, the sangha talks all share one lens), and it contains the author's own sangha material — so it "enthusiastically confirms" the Graeber–Batchelor / dharma-as-mutual-aid thesis that is literally the author's own [S4][S6][S7]. That is corroboration of *content and presence*, not independent verification of the *thesis*.
- **The network is entirely self-describing.** Every public source located — Tuwhiri (the publisher), the Secular Buddhist Network (whose editor, Slott, is also a Tuwhiri author), Bodhi College, and the author's own channel — is *inside* the movement [S11][S22][S26][S28][S29]. No skeptical, scholarly, or outside-the-movement commentary on this specific network was found in either the corpus or the public web. The note records that absence rather than implying the network has been externally scrutinised.
- **The spine of the note is private correspondence between two interested parties.** The relationships (Margolis–Dean, Paul Regan), the forthcoming *Creativity and secular dharma* and its Graeber/Ward/Prichard thesis, the author's backing of the Kickstarter, the books being sent, and the domain history are all sourced to one email thread [S5]. Paul Regan and the forthcoming book/thesis are *email-only* — not publicly attestable; the public record carries only Margolis' broader creativity-and-dharma theme [S15][S18][S19]. These are flagged as self-reported / single-source, which is honest provenance, not a defect. (The correspondent has since reviewed this note and consented to its publication; consent settles the *privacy* question, not the *provenance* one — the single-source flag stands.)
- **Two domain names, one lapsed presence.** The author names `seculardharma.net`; Margolis names `secularbuddhism.net`; both refer to the same lapsed coterie-website, used loosely in the thread [S1][S5]. The present-day web picture (TLS mismatch / no content; unrelated parties in the adjacent namespace) is reported neutrally and is merely *consistent with* reassignment — it does not establish what either domain "is now" [S30][S31].
- **One genuinely independent anchor exists and is used.** Margolis' *public* statement on the Secular Buddhist Network that >700 copies of *What is This?* sold within a week of the Klein transcript is a rare non-private, non-corpus corroboration of the interview's impact, and is cited as the one outside check the research surfaced [S26].
- **Bereavement and political-history claims are the author's own words only.** The 2012 death of his son and the 2015 death of his wife Anita (bracketing the renewed-2014 intensification of his dharma study), and his early Communist Party of Australia membership before gravitating to anarchist groups (the Freedom Collective in Fremantle), are sourced solely to `0-preface/_seed.md` [S1]. Per the privacy constraints on this note, these were captured faithfully and were *not* web-corroborated; they are self-reported and so flagged.
- **Backend caveat (not a corpus gap).** The KB pass found the scholarly RAG synthesis endpoint returning HTTP 502 throughout; all KB findings rest on context-only retrieval, which is the correct Stage-1 primitive and returns raw citable passages with `doc:segment` metadata. Nothing above depends on the synthesis endpoint.
