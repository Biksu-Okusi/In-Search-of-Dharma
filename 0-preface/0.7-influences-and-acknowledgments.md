# 0.7 Influences & Acknowledgments

> ◉ **Public edition.** This note is published in a redacted public edition. Personal contact details, private third-party specifics, and passages resting on the author's private records are excluded, and each exclusion is disclosed where it is made. In this note that rule bites hardest on names: people in public roles are named; private individuals reconstructed from the author's mail archive are described but not named. The private sources themselves (correspondence, family records, the mail archive) are held by the author and are not part of the public repository.

**Question:** Who actually shaped the *In Search of Dharma* essays and the `appliedanthropology`/`seculardharma` knowledgebase? Set the author's acknowledgments down *in proportion* — drawing on his ~1,981-message email archive to reconstruct the real network of people, communities and institutions — and correct the over-prominence that **Ramsey Margolis** acquired across Part 0 simply because his was the richest single source document. Credit in particular **Irfan Kortschak**, the Indonesia-based anthropologist who supplied source texts to the knowledgebase, who appears nowhere in the correspondence — and **Paul Stange**, whose teaching at Murdoch first introduced the author to Java's **Sumarah** movement, a non-religious *kebatinan* path that was his earliest encounter with something like a secular dharma.

## Synthesis

The author states his own acknowledgment plainly in the seed: the essays "would not have been possible without direct input from other people, entities, and non-entities," and "it is impossible to call all my influences out by name" [S2]. This note sets those influences down in proportion — and corrects an imbalance the rest of Part 0 inherits. Because the **Dean–Margolis correspondence** happened to be the richest single document available when Part 0 was researched, **Ramsey Margolis** acquired a prominence across notes 0.1–0.6 out of step with his actual weight as an influence: he is a **2026 correspondent and a publisher** (Tuwhiri), not a formative figure in the author's dharma, and he is absent from the author's *own* acknowledgment [S2][S8]. The author's email archive restores the proportion. The genuinely formative inputs are: **Paul Stange**, the Murdoch University scholar of Javanese mysticism whose teaching gave the author his earliest encounter with something like a secular dharma — Java's **Sumarah** movement, a non-religious *kebatinan* path embedded in Javanese culture, roughly two decades before Batchelor [S13][S14]; **Irfan Kortschak**, an Indonesia-based anthropologist who supplied source texts to the knowledgebase and served as a trusted interlocutor [S1][S3]; **Bodhi College** and its teachers — above all **Stephen Batchelor** — the venue where the author's secular-dharma study and his sangha both formed [S4][S5]; the author's **three-person sangha** and the **seculardharma.net** forum [S6]; a standing **secular-dharma cohort** of fellow practitioners across Europe, North America and Asia [S7][S12]; the **publishing-and-network cluster** (Tuwhiri, the Secular Buddhist Network, Creative Dharma) in which Margolis is one node among Slott, Parks, Higgins and others [S8][S9]; and the **Evolution Institute / Prosocial.world** stream (David Sloan Wilson) that supplies the cooperation-science half of the thesis [S10]. Margolis belongs in this network — but as the publisher who reached out in 2026, not as its hub.

## Findings

### The author's own acknowledgment

The seed sets a deliberately broad, humble frame: "although i have claimed ownership over these essays, they would not have been possible without direct input from other people, entities, and non-entities," and "it is impossible to call all my influences out by name" [S2]. The few he *does* name are the core: "my tiny sangha … comprises myself Elfie, and Rupert," the Bodhi College retreat where the three met ("the 13th of February, 2018 … at the first of six 5-day retreats"), and "the main teacher of this course … Stephen Batchelor, whose book 'After Buddhism' was influential in me taking this course" [S2]. He places this acknowledgment in the same breath as the 2014 intensification of his dharma study, bracketed by the deaths of his son (2012) and his wife Anita (2015) — biography treated in [[0.1-life-and-formation]], named here only because the author himself put acknowledgment and grief in one passage [S2]. The "non-entities" he thanks are the AI tooling and the knowledgebase itself — the method treated in [[0.6-knowledgebase-as-method]]. Tellingly, **Margolis is absent from the author's own acknowledgment**: the prominence he carries elsewhere in Part 0 is an artefact of source-richness, not of the author's reckoning of who mattered [S2].

### Irfan Kortschak — source texts and an anthropologist's ear

By the author's direct account, **Irfan Kortschak** — an Indonesia-based anthropologist — supplied a substantial body of source texts that were incorporated into the `appliedanthropology`/`seculardharma` corpus, and was a trusted interlocutor during the project's formation [S1]. Kortschak appears nowhere in the email archive, and his surname does not surface in the corpus index — the texts he supplied are catalogued under their own titles, not his name — so the supplied-texts credit rests on the author's attestation [S1]. One corpus trace independently confirms the *relationship*: a January-2021 entry in the author's own dharma keep-notes opens by addressing "irfan" directly and asking his opinion about the author's standing within "my bodhi college dharma group" — Kortschak as the confidant the author turned to for counsel on his dharma life (the sensitive personal content is not reproduced here) [S3]. In proportion, his contribution to the knowledgebase that *is* this series' substrate is far larger than that of several figures the rest of Part 0 treats at length.

### Sumarah and Paul Stange — the earliest secular-dharma analogue

The author's *earliest* encounter with something like a secular dharma came not from Buddhism but from Java. By his direct account, **Paul Stange** — his Asian Studies lecturer at **Murdoch University** (where Stange taught from 1980 to 2003 [S15]) and a co-founder of the ACICIS in-country pathway the author travelled to Yogyakarta (see [[0.1-life-and-formation]]) — introduced him to the **Sumarah** movement during his 1990s Indonesian studies [S13]; the Murdoch → Stange → Sumarah link, which the biographical note 0.1 could previously establish only circumstantially, is made direct by this attestation. Sumarah presents itself not as a religion but as "a philosophy of life" and a meditation practice "that originally comes from Java," centred on *sujud* — surrender of "the partial ego to the universal self" — without fixed guru or canon [S14][S15]. (Fittingly, the movement's main English-language website, `sumarah.net`, is itself the author's work: he built it in **1998** while living in Java and still maintains it nearly thirty years later, on essentially the same code — so the site is part of his own long service to Sumarah, not an independent witness to it [S13].) The author practised Sumarah meditation for years, and though it was "inevitably bound to elements [of] traditional Javanese culture, with which I did not always identify," it gave him, roughly two decades before Batchelor, a working example of an ethical and contemplative path *embedded in a culture rather than a creed* [S14]. That reading carries straight into the thesis: in his own "re-defining-dharma" note he lists Sumarah among the "dharmas/adats of many other communities," concluding that "the concept of a dharma is in too many ways inseparable from culture" [S14]. The influence is doubly present in the work — as formation, and as source material: Stange's own Sumarah scholarship (*The Logic of Rasa in Java*; *Modern Javanism: The Evolution of Sumarah*) is folded into the corpus this series queries [S14][S15]. The biographical detail is treated in [[0.1-life-and-formation]]; it is recorded here as the acknowledgment it deserves.

### Bodhi College and its teachers — the formative venue

The institution where the author's study and his sangha both took shape is **Bodhi College**, co-founded by **Stephen Batchelor** (the "main teacher" the seed names) and **Martine Batchelor** [S2][S4]. Its core faculty, as named in the programme mailings, include **Christina Feldman**, **John Peacock**, **Akincano Weber** and **Chris Cullen**, with **Bernat Font** teaching the secular-dharma retreats by 2025; the courses are coordinated by **Aline Petit** and **Signe Glahn** (who co-sign the logistics), with **Tony O'Connor** coordinating the "After Buddhism and Beyond" seminar series [S4]. The college's mailings list retreat venues in England (Sharpham, Devon), the Netherlands (Naarden) and Switzerland (Beatenberg) [S4]; the author's own six-retreat Secular Dharma cohort, however, ran in England (the West Sussex leg he recalls), the Netherlands and Belgium — the Beatenberg listing reflects the college's wider or later programme, not this cohort ([[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]]). The author's first documented step into Batchelor's orbit was not the 2018 retreat but an earlier *online* course: he introduced himself on Batchelor's *Four Great Tasks* course (hosted on Realize Media) as "Gary Dean, from Jakarta, Indonesia … migrated to Indonesia 20 years ago" on **10 November 2016** [S5]. The sangha-forming retreat and Batchelor's *After Buddhism* are treated in full in [[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]]; they are recorded here as acknowledgment.

### The sangha and the seculardharma.net forum

The author's "tiny sangha" is a **three-person** group — **Rupert Bozeat**, **Elfie Klinger** and the author — which grew out of a Bodhi College tutor group and continued as a standing online conversation, hosting the **seculardharma.net** forum (with its monthly "Secular Dharma summary" digests) and the "a secular dharma?" YouTube channel whose cleaned-up transcripts were folded into the knowledgebase [S6]. ▲ An earlier version of this note listed a fourth member; that was wrong. Two independent primary sources give three: the author's own SEED — "my tiny sangha … comprises myself Elfie, and Rupert" — and Rupert Bozeat's own message to the wider list, "Gary, Elfie and I have … continued our small tutor group discussions online" ([[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]]). The fourth name the earlier draft listed belongs to the wider seculardharma.net cohort rather than the sangha — a forum correspondent and reunion coordinator, unnamed in this public edition [S6][S12]; [S6] bundles the sangha and the forum in one entry, which is how the two were run together. The members' identities and the channel-to-corpus pipeline are documented in [[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]] and [[8.4-encoding-dharma-machines]]; this note records the group as a direct, sustained influence on the essays.

### The wider secular-dharma cohort

Beyond the three-person sangha stands the standing peer-circle from Batchelor's "After Buddhism and Beyond" / two-year secular-dharma courses — the people the seed gestures at with "impossible to call all my influences out by name" [S2]. A single 2018 group mailing preserves the roster: well over a dozen fellow practitioners across Europe, North America and Asia, alongside the sangha members, a faculty member and the author [S7]. ▲ *Their names are withheld in this public edition. A course cohort reconstructed from private group mail is precisely the class of third-party disclosure this project's privacy limits exist for: membership of a contemplative course is each member's own to publish, or not. One exception is kept — **Stefano Davide Bettera**, who has written publicly about this very cohort under his own name (see [[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]]).* The circle self-organised annual European reunions (in Belgium and the Netherlands) and ongoing online practice groups, with a handful of members — unnamed here — reliably carrying the reunion and arrival logistics year after year [S7][S12].

### Contemplative teachers and activists in the milieu

The same archive surfaces teachers and organisers who widened the milieu beyond study into practice, hosting and activism: the Amsterdam Yoga Centre as a recurring host venue [S11]; a professional coach who brought an Extinction-Rebellion climate-activism framing into the circle — unnamed here, since a personal political affiliation reconstructed from private mail is not this project's to publish [S11]; and **Stefano Bettera**, the Italian secular-Buddhist organiser and network connector who also appears in the corpus's 2018–19 "Ten Theses" threads and has written publicly of this milieu (see [[0.3-sangha-and-retreat]]) [S11].

### The publishing-and-network cluster — where Margolis belongs

This is the rebalancing made explicit. The cluster the rest of Part 0 reads as "Margolis" is in fact plural: **Tuwhiri** (the publisher — Ramsey Margolis and colleagues), the **Secular Buddhist Network** (**Mike Slott**, editor, author of *Mindful Solidarity*), and **Creative Dharma** (the Margolis–**Brad Parks** newsletter), with **Winton Higgins** and **Jim Champion** behind *After Buddhism: a workbook* and **Linda Modaro** among the more recent Tuwhiri authors [S8][S9]. Margolis writes of himself in a collaborative "we" — "we've successfully published five more books and helped to initiate a couple of newsletters" — an initiator among many, not a solo hub [S8]. His substantive tie to the author is a *single* 2026 correspondence (treated in [[0.4-network-and-publishing]]): genuinely welcome, but recent, and peripheral to the decades of formation this note records. The cluster's detail lives in 0.4; the point here is proportion.

### The science-of-cooperation stream

The cooperation-science half of the author's thesis reaches him partly through the **Evolution Institute / This View of Life / Prosocial.world** stream, whose mailing list he followed across 2018–2021 [S10]. **David Sloan Wilson** — the Institute's president, author of *This View of Life*, founder of Prosocial.world — is named in the author's own thesis "cast" alongside Boehm, Sapolsky and Hrdy (see [[0.5-thesis-evolved-dharma]]), and `prosocial.world` is itself one of the author's knowledgebases [S10]. This is the stream that lets the essays read dharma as "plain natural history" rather than floating piety.

## Tensions, gaps & corpus bias

- **This note corrects a provenance-vs-prominence imbalance; it does not delete the Margolis citations.** The `[S#]` references to the Dean–Margolis correspondence in notes 0.1–0.6 remain valid as *provenance* for specific biographical claims and were intentionally left in place. What is rebalanced is the *framing* prominence, not the sourcing [S2][S8]. Note [[0.4-network-and-publishing]] has been lightly reframed so the network no longer reads as Margolis-centric.
- **The Irfan Kortschak credit rests largely on author attestation.** His name is absent from both the email archive and the corpus index; the supplied-texts contribution is the author's testimony, with one corpus trace (the January-2021 keep-note) confirming the *relationship* but not the texts [S1][S3]. This is honest self-report, flagged as such — and a reminder that the email archive systematically misses influences who reached the author by other channels.
- **The email archive is a single vantage, not an objective influence ranking.** It records who *corresponded with the author*; it over-represents frequent mailers (Bodhi College's bulk programme mailings, the newsletters) and under-represents anyone who shaped the work without emailing him. Most of the corpus's intellectual "cast" — Graeber, Sapolsky, Boehm, Bregman — appears nowhere in this archive, because they influenced the essays through their books, not correspondence (see [[0.5-thesis-evolved-dharma]]).
- **Whole-archive bias matches the project's known lean.** Every cluster sits inside the secular-Buddhist / secular-dharma movement (Bodhi College, the Secular Buddhist Network, Tuwhiri) or the prosocial-evolutionary camp (the Evolution Institute) — the same secular / evolutionary / Western-academic lean named across DD. No traditionalist, non-Western or sceptical correspondents appear; the milieu is self-selected and self-describing.
- **Privacy (public edition).** All personal contact details (email, phone, postal) are stripped throughout and never reproduced. Names are kept only for people in public roles — published authors, an institution's teachers and staff, and figures who have themselves written publicly of this milieu. Private individuals reconstructed from the mail archive (the course cohort, the forum's correspondents, the reunion organisers, a coach) are described but not named: membership of a contemplative course, and a personal political affiliation, are theirs to publish, not this project's. The author's children remain referred to by relationship only — the son who died in 2012 is the standing instance, consistent with the other Part-0 notes. The `0-preface/.mail/` archive is local-only, **private**, and is **not** staged to the shared corpus.
