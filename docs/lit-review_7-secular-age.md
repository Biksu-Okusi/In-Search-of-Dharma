# Literature review — Part 7, *Dharmas in a Secular Age*

**Date:** 2026-07-28 · **Scope:** downloadable full-text sources bearing on `7-secular-age.md`
and its underlying notes (6.1, 6.2, 6.5, 3.1; the grounding notes 3.3/3.4 were covered by the
Part 1 pass and are cross-referenced here only where the need is Part-7-specific).

## Why this review exists

The essay is finished and demographically careful (the Pew corrections in note 6.5 are
exemplary), but an audit of the sources behind it shows a familiar pattern: several
**load-bearing claims rest on excerpt-level, record-level, or promotional sources**, and the
essay's two empirical battlegrounds — the harm done by smartphones/social media, and what the
"nones" are actually doing — are each currently sourced from only one side. Specifically:

| Claim in the essay | Currently sourced to | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Taylor: subtraction stories, nova effect, fragilisation | published excerpts + Wikipedia entry (6.5 S5, admits "excerpt level, not full text") | excerpt-level |
| Asad: "the secular" as constructed formation | publisher page, "summary/metadata level" (6.5 S6) | **already fixed** — full text of *Formations* is in the Part 1 archive (`scratch/sources/part1/1-critical-religion/`) |
| Anscombe: moral "ought" is wreckage of divine law | named in Key Works; no full text behind it in the Part 7 notes | not read |

| Newport: digital declutter as installation ritual | publisher blurb (6.2 S7) | blurb |

## Tier 0 — The six that change the most, soonest

1. **Charles Taylor, *A Secular Age* (Belknap, 2007) — full text, grey upload.**
   <https://archive.org/details/CharlesTaylorASecularAge2007BelknapPressOfHarvardUniversityPress>
   `verified` (item page + 5.2 MB PDF and EPUB listed in metadata) · grey.
   The essay borrows this book's title, stages a formal engagement with it ("on trial"), and
   quotes it three times — all from excerpts. Note 6.5 concedes full text was never consulted.
   The "subtraction stories" definition (p. 22), the nova effect, and the fragilisation thesis
   should be read and page-anchored first-hand before Stage 2 locks the wording.

2. **Bhikkhu Bodhi, "Facing the Great Divide" (*Inquiring Mind* 31.2, Spring 2015).** **OA**
   <https://inquiringmind.com/article/3102_20_bodhi-facing-the-great-divide/> `verified`.
   The most authoritative living Theravāda scholar-translator stating the essay's pivot
   question from the *other* side: secular Buddhism as "a rupture with Buddhist tradition,"
   classical and secular streams named and contrasted by a witness with unmatched canonical
   authority. The essay's Wallace citation gives it one traditionalist; Bodhi is the stronger,
   calmer one. Pair with his scholarly piece: **"What does mindfulness really mean? A
   canonical perspective" (*Contemporary Buddhism* 12.1, 2011)** — grey course copy
   <https://ibc-elibrary.thanhsiang.org/files/public/01_mindfulness_canonical_bhikku_bodhi.pdf>
   `verified` · grey — the source of the "bare attention is not sati" argument the essay's
   "appropriate attention" passage leans on (currently cited to an Access to Insight page).

3. **David McMahan, *The Making of Buddhist Modernism* (OUP, 2008) — full text, grey upload.**
   <https://archive.org/details/makingofbuddhistmodernismdavidmcmahanj_202003_374_T>
   `verified` (0.9 MB PDF listed) · grey.
   The standard genealogy showing that "secular Buddhism" is not a *recovery* of a buried
   practical core but the latest phase of a 150-year hybrid (Romanticism + Protestant
   modernism + scientific rationalism). Directly complicates the essay's Hadot-derived
   "recovery" frame — and the Part 1 summary of Higgins notes he *never cites McMahan*, so
   this is the named missing witness.

4. **Orben & Przybylski, "The association between adolescent well-being and digital technology
   use" (*Nature Human Behaviour* 3, 2019) — accepted manuscript, Oxford ORA.** **OA deposit**
   <https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:5d844350-a359-47d3-b10c-4bfb93ce613b/download_file?file_format=application%2Fpdf&safe_filename=Orben-and-Przybylski-Nature-Human-Behaviour-AAM.pdf&type_of_work=Journal+article>
   `verified` (PDF retrieved); typeset copy <https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/2019-orben.pdf>
   `verified` · grey.
   Specification-curve analysis, n = 355,358: digital-technology use explains at most ~0.4% of
   adolescent well-being variance — comparable, in their famous example, to eating potatoes.
   The strongest single counterweight to the essay's industrial-adversary rhetoric, from the
   sceptical side of the debate the notes admit they lack.

5. **James Williams, *Stand Out of Our Light* (CUP, 2018) — publisher open access.** **OA**
   <https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108453004> `browser` (DOI resolves; cambridge.org bot-blocks
   scripted fetches — one click in a browser).
   The essay quotes Williams twice ("redirecting people away from their wills") via a
   PhilPapers record page. The whole book is legitimately free from the publisher. This is the
   cheapest large upgrade in the whole Part 7 source base.

6. **Émile Durkheim, *The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life* (1912; Swain trans. 1915).**
   **Public domain** <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41360> `verified` (title page checked);
   1915 scan <https://archive.org/details/elementaryformso0000durk_w8x4> `verified`.
   The grounding-without-god argument the essay makes — binding produced by assembly, ritual,
   and the "we," not by the referent of belief — *is* Durkheim's argument, and he is nowhere
   in the project. Sourcing "installation through community" to its classic origin also arms
   the Sunday Assembly section below, which is the empirical test of exactly this claim.

## 1 · Secularization & the nones  (note 6.5)

### The theory shelf

- **Asad, *Formations of the Secular*** — already in the Part 1 archive; note 6.5 should be

- **José Casanova, *Public Religions in the Modern World*** (Chicago, 1994)
  <https://archive.org/details/publicreligionsi0000casa> `lending`.
  The standard disaggregation of "secularization" into three theses (differentiation, decline,

  "the gods have fallen silent" shorthand quietly skips.
- **Grace Davie, *Religion in Britain since 1945: Believing without Belonging*** (Blackwell,

- **David Voas, "The Rise and Fall of Fuzzy Fidelity in Europe" (*European Sociological

  Named here because it is the sharpest published *opponent* of the essay's opening move: on
  Voas's data the middle ground is not seekers assembling private dharmas but "fuzzy" residual
  believers drifting toward stable indifference. See challenge box below.

### The measured nones (all fully OA — primary data)
- **Pew, 2023–24 Religious Landscape Study** <https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/religious-landscape-study-executive-summary/> `verified`
- **Pew, "Spirituality Among Americans" (2023)** <https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/12/07/spirituality-among-americans/> `verified`

- **PRRI, "Religious Change in America"** <https://prri.org/research/religious-change-in-america/> `verified` —
  independent second instrument; useful cross-check on the Pew plateau finding.
- **Linda Woodhead, "The rise of 'no religion' in Britain: The emergence of a new cultural
  majority" (*Journal of the British Academy* 4, 2016).** **OA**
  <https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/1043/11_Woodhead_1825.pdf> `verified`.
  The British Academy lecture: most nones are "not straightforwardly secular" — the closest
  thing in the peer-reviewed literature to the essay's own reading of the seeker, and its best
  published ally.
- **Woodhead, "Intensified Religious Pluralism and De-differentiation" (*Society* 53, 2016).** **OA**
  <https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12115-015-9984-1.pdf> `verified`.
- **Lois Lee et al. (Understanding Unbelief programme), "Atheists and Agnostics Around the
  World" (Kent, 2019).** **OA**
  <https://research.kent.ac.uk/understandingunbelief/wp-content/uploads/sites/1816/2019/05/UUReportRome.pdf>
  `verified`. Six-country study: unbelief is plural, rarely nihilistic, and most unbelievers
  hold "significant existential commitments" — Lee's *Recognizing the Non-religious* (OUP,
  2015) itself is `walled`, but this report carries the same programme's data.
- **Linda Mercadante, "Spiritual Struggles of Nones and 'Spiritual but Not Religious'"
  (*Religions* 11:513, 2020).** **OA CC BY**
  <https://res.mdpi.com/d_attachment/religions/religions-11-00513/article_deploy/religions-11-00513.pdf>
  `verified` (mdpi.com front door bot-blocks; res.mdpi works). 100+ interviews with SBNRs —
  qualitative flesh for the essay's opening portrait.
- **Nadia Beider, "Religious residue" (*British Journal of Sociology* 73, 2022).** **OA**
  <https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-4446.12982> `browser` (Wiley pdfdirect bot-blocks).
  Disaffiliates keep decaying religiosity; lifelong nones have none to keep — and their share
  is growing. Cuts *against* "kept the old need" as a durable claim.
- **Joseph Blankholm, *The Secular Paradox* (NYU Press, 2022)**
  <https://academic.oup.com/nyu-press-scholarship-online/book/44983> `browser` — hosted on
  institutional platforms (OUP / De Gruyter), both bot-blocked; genuine OA status
  **unverified** (it is *not* a Luminos title — that guess in the brief is wrong; Luminos is
  UC Press). Ethnography of how organised nonbelievers end up ambivalently reproducing
  religious forms — the essay's "search for one" thesis observed in the field.
- **Ryan Burge, *The Nones* (Fortress, 2021/2023)** — `walled`; no free or lending copy found.
  His public Substack graphs are citable secondary colour but not a book substitute.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**The essay's seeker may be a minority of the category she fronts.** The opening extrapolates
from the SBNR seeker to the nones at large ("not the absence of a dharma… the search for
one"). Voas's fuzzy-fidelity model and Beider's religious-residue data both read the bulk of
the unaffiliated as *indifferent*, not searching; Pew's own instrument puts US SBNR at 22% —
overlapping but far from coextensive with the 29% unaffiliated, as note 6.5 itself says.
Woodhead, Lee and Mercadante support the essay's reading; Voas and Beider oppose it. The essay
should own that it is taking a side in a live dispute, not reporting a consensus.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**McMahan dissolves the essay's cleanest distinction.** The essay contrasts extraction
(McMindfulness) with reconstruction (Batchelor), and frames the latter, via Hadot, as
*recovery* of a buried practical core. McMahan's genealogy says the "core" being recovered was
itself assembled in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries out of Romantic, Protestant and
scientific materials — i.e. the recovery story is a signature *trope* of Buddhist modernism,
not an escape from it. And Bodhi's canonical-mindfulness paper argues the extraction happened
at the level of *sati* itself. Neither point sinks the essay's argument (which is functional,
not historical), but "it is a recovery, not a theft" should not be asserted as history without
this literature in view.

## 3 · Grounding and binding without God  (essay §"Can it bind?"; notes 3.1, 3.3-adjacent)

- **Ronald Dworkin, *Religion without God* (Harvard, 2013)**
  <https://archive.org/details/religionwithoutg0000dwor> `lending`.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**The Sunday Assembly literature confirms the essay's diagnosis by failure.** The essay calls
the missing "we" the secular dharma's "deepest practical wound," on argument alone. The
field studies supply the evidence: Smith, Bullock and Frost all document the movement's
initial effervescence *and* its chronic difficulty converting attendance into obligation —
and the network's later contraction is public record. This is the strongest empirical support
the "Can it install?" section could cite, and it comes from the experiment's sympathisers.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**The essay's adversary is real but its potency is asserted, not measured.** "We have no
natural defences," "an adversary that gets cleverer every quarter," a world "actively
engineered against" the gathering of a self — all sourced to the critics (van Vugt, Williams,
Harris), none of whom quantifies. The measured literature finds small average associations,
bidirectional causality, and null results for the most intuitive environmental interventions.
Note 6.2 already concedes "the note's adversary is characterised entirely by its critics";
the essay itself should inherit one sentence of that humility — its *phenomenological* claim
(attention scattered, outrage amplified) survives; its implied *causal magnitude* does not yet
have the evidence its rhetoric suggests. Two guardrails for Stage 2: do not let "engineered
to hijack" harden into "demonstrated to harm"; and do not cite the McMindfulness critique
and the harm-scepticism literature in the same breath without noticing they cut in opposite
directions (one says the machine is devastating, the other that its measurable footprint is
small).

## Claims that should be softened or footnoted regardless of acquisition

- **The seeker as the face of the nones** — Voas (fuzzy fidelity) and Beider (religious
  residue) read the majority as indifferent, not searching; Pew's SBNR 22% ≠ unaffiliated 29%.
  A one-clause concession ("many of the nones are simply indifferent; this essay follows the
  seekers among them") would inoculate the opening.
- **"No natural defences" / ever-cleverer adversary** — advocacy-sourced; the measured
  average effects are small and the first school-ban trial is null. Keep the phenomenology,
  drop any implication of demonstrated population-scale psychological damage.
- **Secular translation as "recovery" (Hadot move)** — McMahan's genealogy makes the recovery
  story itself a modernist artefact; state it as the tradition's self-understanding, not as
  settled history.
- **The Euthyphro dispatch** — modified divine command theory (Adams) is the standard reply;
  the believer has not been left speechless since Plato.
- **Anscombe as pure adversary** — her essay recommends the very virtue/flourishing turn the
  essay itself takes three paragraphs later; cite her as diagnosis, not merely objection.
- **"Community… thinned almost to nothing"** — the digital-religion literature (Campbell) and
  the secular-congregation studies show partial, struggling reconstitution, which is exactly
  the essay's own Sumarah-calibrated register elsewhere; align the wording.
