# Jeff Fracher on Gary Dean's ms

- Source: `JF - on Gary Dean’s ms.pdf`
- Reviewer: Jeff Fracher (JF), Tuwhiri editorial board
- Date: not stated (received September 2026)

I appreciated his honesty about how the book came about, including his use of AI! Truly transparent – but might the use of AI turn off some readers?

I really like the way he argues that 'dharmas' (ways of living) aren't sacred mysteries handed down from above, but practical tools that human communities invented to help large groups of strangers get along. This is a fresh way of connecting ideas from religion, anthropology, and biology. Lines up so well with a practical dharma world view.

He also shows that the word originally just meant 'to hold' (like a support beam), which is much more down-to-earth than the mystical Eastern meaning most people imagine. This simple insight makes everything else in the book easier to follow.

Maybe I am being too picky but most of his science comes from a fairly narrow handful of popular authors (Sapolsky, Haidt [a friend of mine from when he was here], and others) who mostly agree with each other. Maybe I, too, am guilty of that? It runs the risk of confirmation bias. Might he have included some voices from scientists who disagree with these ideas?

Some of the case studies get much more careful, loving attention than others. His personal favorite (Sumarah) gets long, detailed treatment, while the Christian/Jewish/Muslim traditions get squeezed into a shorter, more defensive section.

There is a lot of repetition. Because the book was built from separate research notes, the same key points get explained over and over. Also, when he uses the phrase 'essentially contested concept', it lets him avoid having to defend his own definitions as rigorously as he critiques others. Again, am I too picky?

I think 'he doth protest too much' about being neutral. He says repeatedly that he won't rank one way of life above another, but the tone of the writing shows his preferences clearly; he's warmer toward anarchism and Javanese mysticism, cooler toward organised religion. He should either admit this bias more openly or work harder to hide it.

My final concern is whether there is enough of an audience for such a book?

Otherwise, I enjoyed it. The autobiographical opening was fascinating including the description of his personal and spiritual journeys. Clearly, a brilliant autodidact.
