Hello, Ramsey.

I read the first three chapters of the book and skimmed through the rest. I do not intend to read the whole book carefully, cover to cover, because once I get the big picture, I lose interest (I am a 'big picture' person). Having established the background, context, and basic message, extremely well, at the beginning, the book then goes ahead to carefully provide lots of illustrations, practices, applications, supporting real-world examples.

Based on what I have read and skimmed through, here is why I strongly believe that the book should be published by Tuwhiri. (Of course, I have no expertise in the subject matter - I am providing this feedback as an 'outsider').

- Its topic (Secular Dharma) is perfectly aligned with, complementary to, Secular Buddhism, which is the core focus of Tuwhiri as a publisher.
- The book is extremely well written (if fact, beautifully written) - a very easy and pleasant read.
- Beautiful illustrations, dominated by my preferred colours.
- To an outsider like me, it is very informative, interesting, well researched, powerful, and persuasive. Even for insiders, I assume it will provide a great resource and a contribution to the existing knowledge base.
- I love the emphasis on unification rather than division. There is no argument about right or wrong, rather an emphasis on a common humanity searching answers to a shared question, a solution to a shared problem/challenge, how we live in peace with each other - the function that dharma serves is identical across humanity although it comes in numerous manifestations (with various types of machinery to serve that function, to fit different contexts and circumstances).
- The various chapters of the book provide well-researched and credible evidence, from around the world and across history, to support this main thesis.

That is all I have to say - hope it helps - thanks for the opportunity.

Girol
