# Literature review — Part 6, *When Dharmas Go Wrong*

**Date:** 2026-07-28 · **Scope:** downloadable full-text sources bearing on `6-when-dharma-goes-wrong.md`
and its underlying notes (7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.6, 7.7, 6.3, 6.4, with a glance at 5.5).

## Why this review exists

Part 6 is the essay in the series that most needs its sources to be unimpeachable, because it is
the essay that accuses. Every failure it names is attributed to a living tradition, a named
person, or a named regime. An audit of the ~90 sources behind it finds that the **accusations are
better sourced than the analytical frame**: the primary texts and the corpus material are strong,
but a striking number of the essay's *conceptual load-bearers* rest on Wikipedia, publisher
pages, blogs and a YouTube transcript.

Everything below was **link-verified on 2026-07-28** (HTTP status + content-type via
`curl -sIL`; archive.org items checked against `archive.org/metadata` for lending status). Flags
are honest: `verified` = live full text retrieved by scripted fetch; `lending` = archive.org
borrow required; `browser` = open access but bot-blocked to scripted requests (opens in one
click); `walled` = no free copy found.

▲ **Provenance warning.** Many book-length items below are *community uploads of in-copyright
works* on archive.org, monoskop and course sites — live today, removable tomorrow, and not
publisher-authorised OA. Per this project's source policy these are **reading copies only**: read
them, turn them into **attributed summary notes** staged at
`$VECTORDBS/appliedanthropology/workshops/DD/`, and do not add the files to the corpus or the
repo. Items marked **OA** are genuinely open-licensed.

▲ **Stance note.** The essay's organising schema is *the dark twin* — each failure is a strength
over-extended — and it closes the "that's not real Buddhism" exit **first**. This review is
weighted accordingly: sources that document insiders doing harm *authentically within* a
tradition are ranked above sources that quarantine harm as inauthentic. Apologetic literature
that rescues a tradition's essence is of little use here; so is polemic that damns the essence
(the essay already refuses both, via Goldman in 7.1). The corpus's anti-authoritarian lean is
constitutive and is disclosed, not corrected.

## Tier 0 — The six that change the most, soonest

1. **Bhikkhu Anālayo, *The Foundation History of the Nuns' Order* (Hamburg Buddhist Studies 6,
   projektverlag 2016) — full book, OA, publisher-authorised.**
   <https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/foundation.pdf>
   `verified` · **OA**.
   The comparative study of the *garudhammas* and the nuns'-ordination narrative across Pāli,
   Chinese, Sanskrit and Tibetan recensions. The essay's gender paragraph — "an early Buddhist
   rule made every nun, however senior, subordinate to every monk" — is currently Wikipedia-sourced,
   and note 6.4 flags the authenticity question ("absent from Sanskrit and Chinese") without a
   scholarly anchor. This is that anchor, and it is *free and citable*. It is also the single
   biggest upgrade available anywhere in Part 6's source set.

2. **B. R. Ambedkar, *Annihilation of Caste* (1936; annotated 2004 edition) — full text, Columbia
   University.**
   <https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/readings/aoc_print_2004.pdf>
   `verified` · public-domain/institutional host.
   The essay stakes its most hopeful claim on Ambedkar — "the most surgical version of this move
   imaginable" — and sources him to Wikipedia. This is the primary text, including the argument
   that caste is not a division of labour but a division of *labourers*, and that it cannot be
   reformed without destroying the authority of *śāstra*. Companion primary texts at the same
   Columbia project, both `browser` (open in a normal browser; HEAD-blocked to scripts):
   *Castes in India* (1917) <http://www.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_castes.html>
   and *The Buddha and His Dhamma* (1957)
   <http://www.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/>.
   ▲ The last of these is the *test* of the essay's claim that Ambedkar "cut out the doctrine of
   karma": his own text is more careful than the summary — he retains a naturalised, action-consequence
   karma while rejecting rebirth-as-desert. See §4.

3. **Edward W. Said, *Orientalism* (Pantheon 1978) — full text, 397 pp.**
   <https://monoskop.org/images/4/4e/Said_Edward_Orientalism_1979.pdf>
   `verified` · grey upload.
   The essay names Said as the source of the term's critical sense; note 7.2 cites a 900-word
   Conversation explainer. First-hand engagement matters more here than usual, because the essay's
   reflexive turn ("this very project is a prime suspect") only works if the indictment is stated
   in the indicter's own terms.

4. **Robert Jay Lifton, *Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism* (1961) — full book.**
   <https://archive.org/details/ThoughtReformAndThePsychologyOfTotalism>
   `verified` (EPUB present) · grey upload.
   The eight criteria — milieu control, mystical manipulation, the demand for purity, confession,
   sacred science, loading the language, doctrine over person, the dispensing of existence — are
   the analytic vocabulary Part 6 needs for both the cult twin and the engineered-dharma twin, and
   are currently cited from Wikipedia. Lifton's own framing ("the most dangerous direction of the
   twentieth-century mind") is also the closest published statement of the essay's thesis that the
   danger is intrinsic to totality, not to any particular content.

5. **Tom O'Lincoln, *Into the Mainstream: The Decline of Australian Communism* (Stained Wattle
   Press, 1985) — complete book, hosted by permission.**
   <https://www.marxists.org/subject/stalinism/into-mainstream/index.htm>
   `verified` · author-permitted full text.
   Note 7.6 is the most Wikipedia-dependent note in the whole project and says so. This is a
   book-length political history of exactly the arc the note fact-checks: the CPA from WWII to the
   early 1980s, the Moscow break, the SPA and M-L splits, the union base, and the Aarons-era
   turn — freely readable in full. ▲ O'Lincoln writes from the Trotskyist left and reads the CPA's
   post-1968 liberalisation as *rightward drift*, not maturation: he is a hostile witness, which is
   precisely what the note's "moderation vs. franchise-defence" contest needs. He is not a
   psychology-of-disillusionment source and should not be used as one; he is a historian of the
   organisation.

6. **James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (Yale 1998) — full book.**
   <https://archive.org/details/seeing-like-a-state-how-certain-schemes-to-improve-the-human-condition-have-failed-james-scott>
   `verified` (EPUB present) · grey upload.
   Note 7.7 explicitly flags that the four-element schema is carried "via the cited external
   summaries rather than a full-text read." The essay then runs its whole blueprint section on
   that schema and adds a gradient claim Scott does not make. Reading the book is the minimum
   condition for keeping the paragraph.

## 1 · Closing the exit: internal diversity, and religious violence as authentic  (note 7.1)

The essay's opening move is its most important and its worst-sourced. Everything after §"Why
'that's not real Buddhism' is a cop-out" depends on it holding.

- **Diane L. Moore, "Our Method" / the four assertions (Harvard Religion and Public Life).**
  <https://rpl.hds.harvard.edu/what-we-do/our-approach> `browser` (403 to scripted fetch).
  The published institutional statement of the framework the essay attributes to "Scholars at
  Harvard's Religious Literacy Project," currently reaching the project only through a third
  party's video transcript. Moore's monograph — *Overcoming Religious Illiteracy: A Cultural
  Studies Approach* (Palgrave 2007) — is `walled`; her *Religious Education* article
  "Overcoming Religious Illiteracy: Expanding the Boundaries" (2014) is `walled` at T&F. The
  institutional page is the citable free statement.
- **Mark Juergensmeyer, *Terror in the Mind of God*** (UC Press, 3rd/4th eds.)
  <https://archive.org/details/terrorinmindofgo0000juer> `lending` (five copies checked, all
  restricted). The essay's claim — "surveying religious violence across every major tradition,
  found the same grim pattern everywhere" — is a summary of a book nobody on this project has
  read. A borrow session is the honest minimum before that sentence stands.
- **Regina M. Schwartz, *The Curse of Cain: The Violent Legacy of Monotheism*** (Chicago 1997)
  <https://archive.org/details/curseofcainviole0000schw> `lending`. The strongest published
  argument that exclusion is *constitutive* of monotheistic identity rather than incidental to
  it — i.e. the theological version of the essay's dark-twin thesis, and a much better ally than
  the survey literature.
- **Hector Avalos, *Fighting Words: The Origins of Religious Violence*** (Prometheus 2005)
  <https://archive.org/details/fightingwordsori0000aval> `lending`. Avalos's scarce-resource
  theory (religions manufacture scarcities — salvation, sacred space, scripture, group privilege —
  and then fight over them) is a *mechanism* where the essay currently has an analogy. It also
  cuts against the essay: on Avalos's account violence follows from the manufactured scarcity,
  not from in-group warmth over-extended.
- **"What does Buddhism have to do with the ethnic cleansing in Myanmar?", *Lion's Roar***
  <https://www.lionsroar.com/what-does-buddhism-have-to-do-with-the-ethnic-cleansing-in-myanmar/>
  `verified` — currently note 7.1's [S6]; fine as a popular framing, wrong as the anchor for a
  claim about a scholarly literature.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**The essay's parochial-altruism sentence is stronger than its own note.** Part 6 says the
bonding that makes you cherish your people "is, as a matter of evolved psychology, the very
mechanism that primes you to fear and distrust the people beyond the line… the two are a single
instinct, not two." Note 7.1 [S11] already carries Silva & Mace's Northern Ireland field
experiments, which found the outward face *without* the inward face, with socio-economic status
the real driver — and the note revises its thesis accordingly. The essay does not. See §2 for
the source set that lets this be stated properly.

## 2 · In-group closure: the science the essay leans on  (note 7.1, essay §"The fence becomes a wall")

- **Whitehouse, Jong, Buhrmester et al. (2017), "The evolution of extreme cooperation via shared
  dysphoric experiences", *Scientific Reports* 7:44292.** **OA CC-BY**
  <https://www.nature.com/articles/srep44292.pdf> `verified`
  The best free primary source for the mechanism the essay describes: shared painful experience →
  identity fusion → willingness to fight and die. Tested across military veterans, fraternities,
  football fans, martial artists and twins. Replaces the bylineless magazine article that note 7.1
  itself flags as the anchor of the whole cluster.
- **Newson, Buhrmester & Whitehouse (2016), "Explaining Lifelong Loyalty", *PLoS ONE* 11:e0160427.**
  **OA** <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0160427&type=printable>
  `verified`
- **Whitehouse (2018), "Dying for the group: Towards a general theory of extreme self-sacrifice",
  *BBS* 41.** <https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x18000249> `walled` — but the *BBS* format means
  the target article travels with ~30 peer commentaries, several of which contest the fusion
  account directly. Worth an interlibrary attempt; this is the single best "thesis plus its
  critics" package for the essay's boundary claim.
- **Atran, Sheikh & Gómez (2014), "For Cause and Comrade: Devoted Actors and Willingness to
  Fight", *Cliodynamics* 5(1).** **OA** <https://escholarship.org/content/qt6n09f7gr/qt6n09f7gr.pdf>
  `browser` (eScholarship throttles scripted fetches; opens normally).
  And the companion, **Sheikh, Atran, Ginges et al. (2014), "The Devoted Actor as Parochial
  Altruist"**, *Cliodynamics* 5(1) **OA**
  <https://escholarship.org/content/qt7170h7h0/qt7170h7h0.pdf> `browser` — which is, by title and
  by argument, the closest published statement of the essay's exact claim, tested on Sunni, Shia
  and Christian respondents in Lebanon. Its finding is a *qualification* the essay should carry:
  respondents fused with their religion but moralising **universal** values over sectarian ones
  were the **least** willing to support costly sacrifice. Fusion alone does not point the weapon
  outward; the values fused-to decide.
- **Gómez, Vázquez & Atran (2023), "Transcultural pathways to the will to fight", *PNAS* 120.**
  **OA** <https://e-spacio.uned.es/bitstreams/29f2f989-8ff7-4a1e-8521-c43993da26ef/download>
  `verified` (repository deposit) — 31 studies, 9 countries, ~12,000 participants; the scaled-up
  version.
- **Swann & Buhrmester (2015), "Identity Fusion", *Current Directions in Psych. Sci.* 24(1).**
  **OA** <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0963721414551363?download=true> `browser`
  — the compact review, useful for a single citation.
- **Richard Wrangham, *The Goodness Paradox*** (2019) — `lending`/`walled`; no open copy found on
  archive.org under any identifier. The essay's Wrangham paragraph (chimp war-death rates,
  no battlefield self-sacrifice) is currently sourced to a publisher catalogue page. Wrangham's
  peer-reviewed statement of the same argument is the practical substitute; see the challenge below.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**"Chimpanzees kill each other at rates much like ours" is a contested empirical claim, not a
settled one.** The chimp-warfare rate comparison sits inside a long dispute (Wilson et al. 2014
in *Nature* on adaptive intergroup killing, against Ferguson and Sussman's argument that the
rates are artefacts of provisioning and habitat disruption). The essay presents it flatly, and
uses it to carry the section's only note of hope. The hope survives the dispute — Wrangham's
*self-sacrifice* asymmetry is the load-bearing half, and it is much better attested than the
rate comparison — but the sentence should not lean on the rates.

## 3 · The Buddhist counter-example, told honestly  (essay opening; note 7.1)

The essay opens on Wirathu and stakes the whole argument on refusing to quarantine him. That
demands scholarship on Buddhist violence written by Buddhologists, not journalism.

- **Iselin Frydenlund (2021), "Protecting Buddhist Women from Muslim Men: 'Love Jihad' and the
  Rise of Islamophobia in Myanmar", *Religions* 12:1082.** **OA CC-BY**
  <https://res.mdpi.com/d_attachment/religions/religions-12-01082/article_deploy/religions-12-01082-v2.pdf>
  `verified` (use the `res.mdpi.com` mirror; `mdpi.com` 403s scripted fetches).
  Buddhist protectionism in its own vocabulary — *amyo* (race/group), the decline-of-the-dhamma
  frame, the colonial-era genealogy of the threatening-foreign-male trope. This is the essay's
  Wirathu paragraph done properly: the hatred reconstructed as a *dharmic* argument its adherents
  find compelling, not as a lapse.
- **Iselin Frydenlund (2023), "Theorizing Buddhist anti-Muslim nationalism as global
  Islamophobia", *Ethnic and Racial Studies*.** **OA**
  <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/01419870.2023.2268209?download=true> `browser`
  (T&F 403s scripts). Argues Buddhist Islamophobia is globally constituted, not a local aberration —
  which strengthens the essay's refusal to treat Myanmar as an outlier.
- **Saiya & Manchanda (2025), "Monks Behaving Badly: Explaining Buddhist Violence in Asia",
  *International Security* 49(4): 119–.** **OA**
  <https://direct.mit.edu/isec/article-pdf/49/4/119/2523364/isec_a_00510.pdf> `browser` (MIT 403s
  scripts; the DOI resolves). Note 7.1 already links the ISEC article page. Statistical plus
  case-study test of *why* Buddhism turns violent in some states and not others: the answer is
  religion-state integration, and the comparison set is Myanmar / Sri Lanka / Thailand / Singapore.
  This is a structural rival to the essay's psychological explanation, and a good one.
- **Jerryson & Juergensmeyer (eds.), *Buddhist Warfare*** (OUP 2010)
  <https://archive.org/details/buddhistwarfare0000unse> `lending`. The standard collection;
  Jerryson's Thailand chapter and Victoria's Zen-at-war material are the strongest "insiders doing
  harm authentically" evidence available.
- **Stanley J. Tambiah, *Buddhism Betrayed? Religion, Politics, and Violence in Sri Lanka***
  (Chicago 1992) <https://archive.org/details/buddhismbetrayed0000tamb> `lending`. ▲ Note the
  question mark in the title: Tambiah's book is *itself* an argument about whether the
  "betrayal" framing is available — the exact question the essay's opening section decides. It
  belongs in the argument, not just the bibliography.
- **Tessa Bartholomeusz, *In Defense of Dharma: Just-War Ideology in Buddhist Sri Lanka***
  (RoutledgeCurzon 2002) — `walled`; no copy found on archive.org, monoskop, or any repository.
  The title alone states the essay's thesis, and the book reconstructs a Buddhist *just-war*
  argument from Sinhala sources. Highest-value unobtainable item in this review. Her earlier
  *JBE* article-length treatments of the same material are OA and should be substituted.

## 4 · Caste, karma, and dharma-sanctified hierarchy  (notes 7.3, 6.3)

- **Ambedkar, *Annihilation of Caste*** — see Tier 0.
- **David Mosse (2019), "The Modernity of Caste and the Market Economy", *Modern Asian Studies*
  53(4).** **OA** <https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FD0555C7F8B2232FFDD1CC6995D218B8/S0026749X19000039a.pdf/div-class-title-the-modernity-of-caste-and-the-market-economy-div.pdf>
  `verified`. Caste as a live economic mechanism in liberalised India — network, opportunity-hoarding,
  labour-market discrimination — explicitly *against* the "enclosure" of caste in the non-modern
  realm of religion. Directly relevant to note 6.3's varṇa section and to the essay's pairing of
  caste with meritocracy.
- **Nicholas B. Dirks, *Castes of Mind: Colonialism and the Making of Modern India*** (Princeton
  2001) <https://archive.org/details/castesofmindcolo0000dirk> `lending`. The standing argument
  that caste *as a single systematic order* is substantially a colonial-administrative artefact —
  which complicates the essay's move from Manu straight to lived caste.
- **M. N. Srinivas, *The Dominant Caste in Rampura*** (OUP 1987)
  <https://archive.org/details/the-dominant-caste-in-rampura> `verified` (open, EPUB). Srinivas's
  *Caste in Modern India* <https://archive.org/details/casteinmodernind0000srin> and *Social
  Change in Modern India* <https://archive.org/details/socialchangeinmo0000srin> are both
  `lending`. Srinivas supplies "dominant caste" and "sanskritisation" — mobility mechanisms that
  show the system was never as static as the karmic-desert story implies.
- **Louis Dumont, *Homo Hierarchicus*** (grey scan, open)
  <https://archive.org/details/kaox-homo-hierarchicus-the-caste-system-and-its-implica> `verified` ·
  grey. The classic structuralist account of purity/pollution as a religious ideology of hierarchy —
  the position Dirks and Mosse write against. Read as the thesis, not the settled view.
- **San, Drechsler & Shakya (2023), "Buddhism, Wealth, and Privilege: Ambedkar and Habermas",
  *Religions* 14:1057.** **OA CC-BY**
  <https://res.mdpi.com/d_attachment/religions/religions-14-01057/article_deploy/religions-14-01057.pdf>
  `verified`. Reads Ambedkar's Buddhist essays against a Theravāda account of merit and inherited
  wealth — useful for testing how much of the karma-as-desert doctrine Ambedkar actually deleted.
- **Olivelle, *Manu's Code of Law*** (OUP 2005) — **already in `scratch/sources/part1/`**;
  cross-reference only. This is the *Manusmṛti* the gender and caste sections both quote from
  Wikipedia.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**"He had earned it cosmically, by his conduct in a previous life, and his suffering was
therefore just" is the *text's* account, not necessarily the *lived* one.** A long ethnographic
argument (Moffatt's consensus thesis versus Deliège's and Mosse's counter-evidence) finds that
Dalit communities frequently do *not* internalise karmic desert as an explanation of their own
position, and maintain counter-narratives of origin and injustice. The essay's sentence describes
what the dharma *told* people; it should not be read as what people believed. That distinction
matters especially because the essay's next move is Ambedkar — whose whole existence is evidence
against the internalisation claim.

## 5 · Power: hegemony, symbolic power, productive power  (note 7.3)

  <https://archive.org/download/selections-from-the-prison-notebooks-of-antonio-gramsci/Selections%20from%20the%20Prison%20notebooks%20of%20Antonio%20Gramsci.pdf>
  `verified` · grey. Replaces the SEP-mediated citation for a claim the essay makes in Gramsci's

- **Pierre Bourdieu, *Language and Symbolic Power*** (Polity 1991)
  <https://monoskop.org/images/4/43/Bourdieu_Pierre_Language_and_Symbolic_Power_1991.pdf>

- **Michel Foucault, *Discipline and Punish*** (Vintage 1995)
  <https://monoskop.org/images/4/43/Foucault_Michel_Discipline_and_Punish_The_Birth_of_the_Prison_1977_1995.pdf>
  `verified` · grey. Covers disciplinary power and docile bodies.
  ▲ **The essay's Foucault paragraph is actually about *pastoral* power**, which is not in

- **Max Weber, "The Sociology of Charismatic Authority" and "The Routinization of Charisma", in

  `browser` (403 to scripts). Monin's own later review of the literature he founded.
- **Blanken, van de Ven, Zeelenberg & Meijers (2014), "Three Attempts to Replicate the Moral
  Licensing Effect", *Social Psychology* 45(3).** <https://doi.org/10.1027/1864-9335/a000189>

- **Simbrunner & Schlegelmilch (2017), "Moral licensing: a culture-moderated meta-analysis",

  <https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs11301-017-0128-0.pdf> `browser` — d ≈ 0.32,

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**"Psychologists have documented what they call moral licensing" is a replication-crisis claim
stated as settled.** Mullen & Monin's own review concludes that consistency and licensing are
both observable and that which one appears depends on construal level, progress-vs-commitment
framing and identity threat; they further note that many studies used "donut designs" lacking a
neutral baseline, leaving it *ambiguous whether licensing was observed at all*. Blanken et al.
failed to replicate the canonical demonstration three times. The effect is real but small and
condition-dependent. The essay's "the community certain of its own righteousness is the community
best able to excuse its own cruelty" is a good argument that does not need the experimental
finding to carry it — and the finding, as cited, cannot.

Purity spirals: note 7.1 correctly marks these as the author's extrapolation `[A]`. No published
source was found that measures them; the honest options are to keep the `[A]` marking into Stage 2
or drop the claim to an illustration.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**"Somewhere around one person in a hundred" is a trade-book figure, not an epidemiological one.**
The ~1% prevalence comes from Hare's popular writing. The best community estimate (Coid et al.,
British household survey, 2009) puts PCL-R-defined psychopathy at roughly 0.6% of the general
population, and the construct itself is contested — the boundary between psychopathy and
antisocial personality disorder, the factor structure of the PCL-R, and its cross-cultural
validity are all live disputes. Nothing in the essay's argument depends on the exact figure: the
claim it needs is only that such people exist in small, non-zero numbers and that dharmas are good
hunting grounds for them. Soften to "a small fraction of any population" and the paragraph
survives untouched.

## Claims that should be softened or footnoted regardless of acquisition

- **"The two are a single instinct, not two"** (parochial altruism) — contradicted by the essay's
  own note ([S11], Silva & Mace) and qualified by Sheikh & Atran's finding that universalist
  values decouple fusion from hostility.
- **"Chimpanzee cousins, who kill each other at rates much like ours"** — a contested rate
  comparison doing rhetorical work the self-sacrifice asymmetry can do on its own.
- **"Psychologists have documented what they call moral licensing"** — three failed replications
  and a moderator-dependent meta-analysis; the "donut design" problem means the base finding is
  partly an artefact of missing controls.
- **"He had earned it cosmically… his suffering was therefore just"** — the text's account, not
  the demonstrated lived one; Ambedkar's existence is the essay's own counter-evidence.
- **"An early Buddhist rule made every nun subordinate to every monk"** — carry the authenticity
  caveat the note carries and the essay drops.
- **"Once God fell away, slowly curdled into the modern faith in meritocracy"** — a two-stage
  historical thesis on the authority of one popular-philosophy book. Attribute to Sandel in-text.
- **"Roughly one person in a hundred"** — trade-book figure; community estimates are lower and
  the construct is contested.
- **The blueprint gradient ("the body count rises with the depth of the erasure")** — three
  confounded cases, as note 7.7 admits and the essay does not; and North Korea is the case that
  did not die with its decreer.
- **"The tidy nouns… are to a considerable degree nineteenth-century European inventions"** —
  state with the Lorenzen/Truschke/McMahan qualification the project already owns.
- **"McMindfulness"** — in quotation marks with no attribution; the coiners are Purser & Loy and
  both statements are free.
