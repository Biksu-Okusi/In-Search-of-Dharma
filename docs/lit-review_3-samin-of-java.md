# Literature review — Part 3, *The Dharmas of the Samin of Java*

**Date:** 2026-07-28 · **Scope:** downloadable full-text sources bearing on `3-samin-of-java.md`
and its underlying note (4.1), covering the Samin movement / Sedulur Sikep, *Agama Adam*, the
BKI colonial-era canon, the Javanese-culture baseline, the Scott comparative frame, and the
Kendeng anti-cement resistance (2006–2026).

## Why this review exists

The essay is in good shape, and note 4.1 is unusually candid about its own evidence. But an audit
of its Sources list shows that several **load-bearing claims rest on a single translated YouTube
transcript, on abstract-only records, or on a Wikipedia URL** — and one attribution is wrong.
Specifically:

> **Headline finding of this pass:** the essay's *opening scene* — the deadpan dialogue on the
> forest road — could not be found in any of the three canonical full texts (Benda & Castles 1969,
> Korver 1976, Shiraishi 1990, all searched end-to-end). Indonesian popular sources carry variant
> versions of the same joke ("From where?" "*Saka mburi*" — from behind; "Where to?" "To the
> front"). Until an archival attestation surfaces, the dialogue should be treated as **emblematic
> folklore about** the wong sikep, not a documented incident. What the colonial file *does*
> contain is just as good: Benda & Castles quote a patih's interrogation in which a Saminist
> answers "**Wong Sikep knows no taxes**" and stays calm when slapped for it — a documented
> deadpan, with the *wong sikep* self-designation already in it, decades before Dangir.

Everything below was **link-verified on 2026-07-28** (HTTP status + content-type; the four
canonical texts downloaded and text-searched). Access flags are honest: `verified` = live full
text retrieved; `lending` = archive.org borrow required; `browser` = OA but bot-blocked to
scripted fetches; `walled` = no free copy found.

▲ **Provenance warning.** The Brill BKI articles are genuinely publisher-OA (the journal is

`$VECTORDBS/appliedanthropology/workshops/DD/`, never redistribute. The Indonesian OJS journals

## Tier 0 — The five that change the most, soonest

1. **Victor T. King, "Some observations on the Samin movement of North-Central Java", *BKI*
   129(4) (1973): 457–481 — full text.**
   <https://brill.com/downloadpdf/journals/bki/129/4/article-p457_2.pdf>
   `verified` (downloaded this pass; browser UA required) · publisher OA.
   **Entirely absent from the note.** The first comparative-theoretical treatment: King reviews
   Benda & Castles and The Siauw Giap and argues for a **multi-causal** analysis (social, economic,
   political, religious) against any single framing. This is the missing middle term between the
   note's three "contested classification" poles, and it engages the relative-deprivation and
   millenarian literatures (Cohn, Wertheim, Aberle) directly. Companion piece: King, "Status,
   economic determinism and monocausality: more on the Samin", *BKI* 133 (1977): 350–354,
   DOI [10.1163/22134379-90002617](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134379-90002617) — same OA journal,
   but Brill's PDF endpoint returned **500** on every attempt this pass; `browser` (retry, or via
   the DOI in a browser).

2. **A. P. E. Korver, "The Samin movement and millenarism", *BKI* 132(2/3) (1976): 249–266 —
   full text.** <https://brill.com/downloadpdf/journals/bki/132/2-3/article-p249_4.pdf>
   `verified` (downloaded this pass) · publisher OA.
   The note cites this [S3] from a **ResearchGate landing page**; the essay leans on "the
   contested millenarian reading" and on Shiraishi's demolition of it. Reading Korver first-hand
   is the fair-trial requirement: he explicitly measures the movement against Cohn, Talmon and
   Hobsbawm rather than merely asserting salvationism.

3. **Arif Rohman, "Rumours and Realities of Marriage Practices in Contemporary Samin Society",
   *Humaniora* (UGM) 22(2) (2010): 113–124, and Part II.**
   <https://www.neliti.com/publications/11603/rumours-and-realities-of-marriage-practices-in-contemporary-samin-society>
   `browser` (Neliti/SSRN/Academia all JS-wall scripted fetches; page confirmed live).
   Corrects the note's **misattribution to Amrih Widodo**, and matters substantively: Rohman
   argues Samin marriage practice shows "an egalitarian respect for women" and debunks the
   virginity-test and wife-swap rumours — a direct, published counterweight to the
   "women subordinate" claim the note currently carries from an abstract [S14]. The gender
   paragraph of note 4.1 should not stand on one side of an intra-Indonesian debate it has not
   read.

4. **Yvonne Kunz, Jonas Hein & Mokh Sobirin, "Beyond Protection, toward Respect: Struggle for
   Environmental Justice in the Kendeng Mountains", *Society & Natural Resources* 37(1) (2023).** **OA**
   <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/08941920.2023.2267497?needAccess=true>
   `browser` (T&F 403s scripted fetches; OA confirmed via OpenAlex).
   The strongest peer-reviewed English treatment of the Kendeng struggle — and it documents the
   **second front the essay never mentions**: the fight against a subsidiary of Germany's
   **HeidelbergCement AG** in Pati, alongside PT Semen Indonesia in Rembang. Also carries the
   Sedulur Sikep ontology (the mountain as a living body) and the epistemic-justice reading of
   the EIA battle. Sequel with the same team: Flemmer, Gresz & Hein, "What *Is* at Stake? The
   Ontological Dimension of Environmental Conflicts", *S&NR* (2024)
   <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/08941920.2024.2329888?download=true> `browser`.

5. **Ronald Adam & Zainal Abidin Bagir, "The Indigenous Politics of Justice: The Case of the
   Sedulur Sikep Movement in Central Java", *Jurnal Kawistara* 12(1) (2022).** **OA**
   <https://jurnal.ugm.ac.id/kawistara/article/download/67991/34823>
   `verified` (`application/pdf`).
   Four months of field observation living with wong sikep households in Baturejo (Sukolilo, Pati),
   20 interviews — the closest thing in the free literature to **current interview-based
   ethnography**, showing the inseparability of the spiritual and political-economic that the
   essay asserts. Together with Shiraishi's Dangir, it brackets the "outsiders' windows" problem
   at both ends of the century.

## 1 · The colonial-era canon (the BKI file, and what it actually says)

- **Benda & Castles, "The Samin movement", *BKI* 125(2) (1969): 207–240.**
  <https://brill.com/downloadpdf/journals/bki/125/2/article-p207_2.pdf> `verified` · publisher OA
  (browser UA required). Already read in full by the project [S2] via the same Brill PDF; the
  note's link is a JSTOR stable URL (`walled` scripted) — swap it for Brill.
  Text-search findings this pass: born "**probably in 1859**" near Randublatung; "an **ordinary
  peasant**, the owner of 3 bau (about 5 acres) of ricefield"; the 1907 following "**supposedly…
  3,000**"; forestry conversion to *houtvesterijen* "**from 1897**"; died "in exile at **Padang**,
  Sumatra, in 1914"; the ngoko insistence and the *elmoe nabi adam* passages confirmed; the
  interrogation scene ("Wong Sikep knows no taxes") at the patih's office; Samin's own denial of
  the Ratu Adil prophecies under interrogation ("I know nothing about it and have never told them
  this") — a primary-source anticipation of Shiraishi's spy-scepticism. **Not present:** the
  forest-road catechism, the word *moksha*, Sawahlunto.
- **Takashi Shiraishi, "Dangir's Testimony: Saminism Reconsidered", *Indonesia* 50 (1990): 95–120.**
  <https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/627cb516-54d0-47e4-b1e5-1696c3a86335/content>
  `verified` (re-downloaded and text-searched this pass) · Cornell eCommons, institutionally OA.
  The project's own [S8]; URL still live. Also confirmed **not** to contain the forest dialogue.
- **King 1973 and 1977** — see Tier 0.
- **The Siauw Giap, "The Samin and Samat movements in Java: two examples of peasant resistance",
  *Revue du Sud-Est Asiatique* 1967(2): 303–310 and 1968(1): 107–113; plus "The Samin movement
  in Java: complementary remarks", *RIMA* 3 (1969).** `walled` — no digital copy found of any of
  the three; the journal is not digitised. A blogspot reprint of uncertain fidelity exists
  (<http://nusantaraislam.blogspot.com/2012/10/the-samin-and-samat-movements-in-java.html>, grey
  HTML). King 1973 summarises his arguments and is the practical access route.
- **J. E. Jasper, *Verslag betreffende het onderzoek in zake de Saminbeweging* (1918).**
  `walled` — the foundational colonial rapport is **not on Delpher** or any repository found this
  pass; it survives archivally (and is quoted at length in Benda & Castles, who had it in hand;
  their nn. 26, 37). Second-hand access via B&C is currently the only route. ▲ B&C date Jasper's
  appointment to **1917** (Assistant-Resident of Tuban); the note's "Jasper 1918 rapport" label
  is the report date, not the investigation date.
- **Sartono Kartodirdjo, *Protest Movements in Rural Java* (OUP/ISEAS, 1973).**
  <https://archive.org/details/protestmovements0000kart> `lending`. The comparative Javanese
  frame (he treats Saminism among agrarian radicalism cases). No free copy.
- **George McTurnan Kahin, *Nationalism and Revolution in Indonesia* (1952).**
  <https://archive.org/details/george-mc-turnan-kahin-1952-2003-nationalism-revolution-in-indonesia>
  `verified` · grey upload (an unrestricted community copy; the lending copy is
  `nationalismrevol0000kahi`). Kahin's brief Samin passage is the classic *nationalist-prehistory*
  reading — useful as a foil, exactly the retrospective enlistment the note's romanticisation
  flag warns against.
- **Takashi Shiraishi, *An Age in Motion: Popular Radicalism in Java, 1912–1926* (1990)** —
  `lending` only; the *Indonesia* article carries the Samin-specific argument regardless.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**The canon does not know the essay's opening scene.** See the headline finding above. The
strongest documented equivalents are (a) B&C's "Wong Sikep knows no taxes" interrogation
exchange, and (b) Dangir's punning testimony in Shiraishi. If the essay keeps the forest-road
dialogue — and as an *emblem* it is true to everything documented — it should be introduced as
the story the tradition tells, not as an event on the record. The underlying research note should
downgrade the claim from [S1]-sourced fact to flagged folklore.

## 2 · The forestry state and the 1897 enclosure  (Peluso's frame, free substitutes)

- **Nancy Lee Peluso, *Rich Forests, Poor People* (UC Press, 1992)** —
  <https://archive.org/details/richforestspoorp0000pelu> `lending` (both scans restricted). The

- **K. Mizuno, H. S. Hasibuan, M. Okamoto et al., "Creation of the State Forest System and Its

  (2023): 47–73.** **OA — official**
  <https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/seas/12/1/12_47/_pdf> `verified` (`application/pdf`).
  A modern, fully-OA scholarly statement of exactly the enclosure frame the note borrows from
  Peluso: the Forest Service's rise, criminalised customary use, the permit system nobody used.
  The best *citable* replacement while Peluso remains lending-only.
- **"Samin dan Kehutanan Jawa Abad XIX", *Jurnal Sosiologi Reflektif* 10(1) (2016).** **OA**
  <http://ejournal.uin-suka.ac.id/isoshum/sosiologireflektif/article/download/1140/1050>
  `verified`. Indonesian-language treatment tying the Samin emergence to Daendels-era forestry,
  the 1865 forestry regulation and the 1870 Agrarian Law — deepens the 1897 date into a
  century-long build-up. ▲ OpenAlex mangles its author metadata; take authorship from the PDF.
- Benda & Castles' own forestry section (pp. 222 ff.) is verified in hand and carries the 1897
  *houtvesterijen* conversion and the "45,000 forest offences a year" figure.

## 5 · Agama Adam and the living Indonesian scholarship  (the [S12]-level layer, now readable)

The Indonesian diamond-OA literature on the community is large and almost entirely free. The
richest single vein is **Moh Rosyid** (IAIN Kudus), whom the note cites only "at metadata level":

- **Moh Rosyid, "Komunitas Samin: Agama Adam dan Ajarannya", *Hanifiya* 6(2) (2023).** **OA**
  <https://journal.uinsgd.ac.id/index.php/hanifiya/article/download/16620/9589> `verified`.
  Agama Adam's actual content from fieldwork: Adam as first creature of God (*Yai*), prayer,
  *semedi* at midnight in the *sanggar pamujan*, fasting, oral regeneration — far more granular
  than anything currently under [S1]/[S12].
- **Mohammad Rosyid, "Studi Komparatif Konsep Ketuhanan Islam dan Agama Adam", *Ulumuna* 16(2)
  (2012).** **OA** <https://ulumuna.or.id/index.php/ujis/article/download/210/193> `verified`.
  The theistic content of Agama Adam (God as *Yai*) — directly relevant to the essay's "not
  secular" honest pause, and a corrective to any drift toward reading the wong sikep as
  proto-secularists.
- **Mukodi & Afid Burhanuddin, "Islam Abangan dan Nasionalisme Komunitas Samin di Blora",
  *Walisongo* 24(2) (2016).** **OA**
  <http://journal.walisongo.ac.id/index.php/walisongo/article/download/1086/pdf> `verified`.
- **Nazar Nurdin & Ubbadul Adzkiya, "Tradisi Perlawanan Kultural Masyarakat Samin", *Jurnal
  Sosiologi Agama* 15(1) (2021).** **OA**
  <http://ejournal.uin-suka.ac.id/ushuluddin/SosiologiAgama/article/download/2676/1793> `verified`.
  Samin Kudus today: closed resistance (explicitly via Scott) turning open — learning to write,
  mastering population administration, incorporating as a legal entity. The community *adapting
  its refusal*, which the essay's "same spine, new century" line needs.
- **Nawiyanto & Eko Crys Endrayadi, "The Saminist Movement of the Kendeng Mountains Complex of
  Java during the Dutch Colonial and Indonesia's Reform Eras", *Tawarikh* 9(1) (2017).**
  <https://journals.mindamas.com/index.php/tawarikh/article/view/821> `browser` (OJS serves the
  PDF through an HTML viewer; page live). The colonial-to-Reformasi bridge in one historian's
  arc — precisely the essay's own trajectory.
- **Lestari, Fibiona & Rakhmanto, "Resistance, Identity, and Colonial Perspectives:
  Reinterpreting Saminism…", *Local History & Heritage* 6(1) (2026).** **OA**
  <https://jurnal.medanresourcecenter.org/index.php/LHH/article/download/2062/2214> `verified`.
  Brand-new archival re-reading of the colonial discourse ("backward, irrational") as
  justification for control — the historiographic self-awareness the note's Shiraishi section
  argues for, now in the current Indonesian literature.
- **Sukirno, "Diskriminasi Pemenuhan Hak Sipil Bagi Penganut Agama Lokal", *Adminitrative Law &
  Governance Journal* 1(3) (2018).** **OA**
  <https://ejournal2.undip.ac.id/index.php/alj/article/download/3804/2123> `verified` URL from
  OpenAlex (not curl-checked this pass; Undip's other endpoint verified). The **KTP religion
  column after the 2016 Constitutional Court ruling** (No. 97/PUU-XIV/2016) — the legal update to
  Widodo's New Order story: the state's naming-and-numbering pressure, and its partial retreat.
- **Setiadi, Saraswati & Rosyid, "Geger Sikep", *Komunitas* 9(1) (2017)** — the note's [S11].
  UNNES returns **403 to every scripted fetch** (view page and PDF alike): `browser`. Same for
  the two education studies under [S13] and **Lestari & Wijayanti, "Social Conservation of Local
  Wisdom on Samin Tribes at Kudus", *Komunitas* 12(1) (2020)**. All are OA in a browser.

## Claims that should be softened or footnoted regardless of acquisition

- **The forest-road dialogue** — not in Benda & Castles, Korver, or Shiraishi (full-text searched);
  present only in popular Indonesian tradition in variant forms. Tell it as the tradition's
  emblem, not as an event; or source its earliest attestation if one exists.
- **"Exiled to Sawahlunto… died in 1914"** — B&C say only "died in exile at Padang, Sumatra";
  Sawahlunto rests on the transcript [S1]. (Compatible — Sawahlunto is inland of Padang — but the
  specific claim is transcript-level.)
- **"Three and five thousand followers by 1907"** — B&C: 772 counted in 1903; "supposedly" 3,000
  in 1907; the 5,000 is the transcript's Dutch-tax-collector figure. "Perhaps three thousand, by
  the officials' own uncertain count" would be honest.
- ***Moksha*** — the Sanskrit term is the tradition's later (and the transcript's) gloss; B&C
  record only the belief that Samin lived and would return. The essay's "a detail that will
  matter later" survives either way.
- **The gender paragraph** (note 4.1) — the "subordinate" finding is abstract-sourced and now has
  a published, full-length contrary (Rohman 2010: egalitarian marriage practice, rumours
  debunked). State the disagreement; also fix the **Rohman/Widodo misattribution** in [S14].
- **The Kendeng enemy** — not only PT Semen Indonesia: the parallel and still-live fight in Pati
  is against an Indocement subsidiary of HeidelbergCement (Kunz et al. 2023; AP 2025). The
  essay's singular "a cement company" flattens a two-front, transnational struggle.

  interrogation records, Jasper's inquiry), but the specific catechism it introduces is not in
