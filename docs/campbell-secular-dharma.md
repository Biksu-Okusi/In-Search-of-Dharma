# Campbell against *secular dharma*

*In Search of Dharma · corpus note · 8 September 2026*

What the four *Masks of God* volumes, newly added to the appliedanthropology knowledgebase, do to the book's central claim.

> **The query.** I have just added works of Joseph Campbell to the appliedanthropology knowledgebase. How does Campbell's work challenge the idea of a secular dharma, as expressed in the book *In Search of Dharma*?

Campbell is not the traditionalist opponent the book braces for. He concedes two of his four functions of myth to the secular outright: cosmology now belongs to science ("for this we all turn today... not to archaic religious texts but to science"), and social order is now human convention ("our laws are not from God or from the universe, but from ourselves; are conventional, not absolute" `Occidental-Mythology:631`). That second line is "made, not found" in Campbell's own words.

His challenge cuts elsewhere, at six joints in the book, ordered below by how much weight each carries. Twenty-four chunks were read across all four volumes; *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* is cited inside them but is not in the knowledgebase.

## The four-function scorecard

Campbell's own instrument, run against the book's secular dharma. Cleaner than asking whether he "challenges" it.

| Function of myth | Campbell's term | Book's secular dharma | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Mystical | Awe before the mystery of being; "vitalizing all" | Absent from the four features and the coda's contents list; door ajar in Part 8's "reverence in the natural world" | **Fails** |
| 2 · Cosmological | An image of the universe, now supplied by science | Naturalist without being scientistic | **Passes** |
| 3 · Sociological | A "system of sentiments" binding the individual to the group | The book's whole definition lives here; Campbell agrees it is now human-made | **Passes** |
| 4 · Pedagogical | Carrying the individual through the life stages by ordeal | Has the funeral; lacks the ordeal | **Contested** |

## Challenge 1: The definition leaves out the function that "vitalizes all"

Campbell's first function of mythology is "eliciting and supporting a sense of awe before the mystery of being"; it "antecedes and defies definition"; "talk and teaching cannot produce it. Nor can authority enforce it" `Occidental-Mythology:630`. The book's four features (comprehensive, identity-constituting, oriented to the good, reflectively self-aware) and the coda's contents list (values, ethics, practices, stories, duties, community, identity, discipline, transmission) contain nothing that answers to this. "Stories: why life is this way" is cosmological explanation, not the numinous.

Campbell would say the book has defined dharma by his third function (Radcliffe-Brown's "system of sentiments," which he quotes approvingly `Primitive-Mythology:112`) and his fourth, and dropped the first. He has a name for that move. On Frazer's reading of regicide as "a practical measure, practically conceived," he writes:

> We cannot assume that early man... was rather a sort of primitive social scientist than a true subject of numinous seizure. `Oriental-Mythology:52`

The book's cooperation theory of dharma (Curry, Christakis, Tinbergen's function/mechanism split) is Frazer's fallacy in evolutionary dress, on his reading. Part 1 just imported Frazer directly, so the objection lands on the same page.

**Book's position.** Door ajar. Part 8's post-traditional dharma is "naturalist without being scientistic, finding genuine reverence in the natural world," and Batchelor's "ultimate concern" (Tillich) is Campbell's first function in secular dress. Neither is in the definition. Campbell's point is that a dharma can pass all four features and be dead: "faith in science of modern Philistia" is, for him, as blind as faith in scripture `Creative-Mythology:797`.

## Challenge 2: Found, not made: living symbols cannot be built to order

This is the direct collision with Part 8's spine.

> Such signs cannot be invented. They are found. Whereupon they function of themselves. And those who find them are... poets and creative artists. `Occidental-Mythology:630`

His typology is poet, prophet ("poetry overdone"), priest ("poetry done to death"). A public constitution voted on by a thousand adults, an Earth Charter, a corpus of sourced notes poured into a machine: none of these is poet or prophet. Campbell says of scholarship that it "has nothing to do with creative life and less than nothing with... creative myth" and that "for scholars as a race such work affords a career" `Creative-Mythology:43`.

He does allow creation, but by one route only: an individual "has had an experience of his own... which he seeks to communicate through signs; and if his realization has been of a certain depth... his communication will have the value and force of living myth, for those... who receive and respond to it of themselves, with recognition, uncoerced" `Creative-Mythology:0`. Experience first, symbol second, reception uncoerced. Part 8's construction runs the other way: function first, then a document, then installation.

**Book's position.** The outcome is half-conceded without the mechanism. Part 8 admits the secular substitutes "have not come close to matching the pull of the communities they set out to replace" and that "the binding power travelled with the very supernatural warrant the secular versions renounce." Campbell supplies the why: the warrant was never the metaphysics but the numinous the metaphysics carried, and a dharma built from its sociological function has nothing to carry it in.

## Challenge 3: Installation needs the ordeal, and a book cannot do it

Part 7's installation rule (whole bundle, this-worldly replacement, practised way of life) is compatible with Campbell up to a point. Both say practice over doctrine. But Campbell's account of installation is depth-psychological, not habituational: initiation is "the caldron" where universal infantile imprints are "cowed, broken, recoordinated to a larger format" through "an extremely vivid, increasingly frightening and unforgettable series of controlled experiences" `Primitive-Mythology:111` `Primitive-Mythology:112`. The second birth is "well worth the pain and fright." The book's bundle has no ordeal in it.

His diagnosis of the seeker with her app and her Stoic sayings is already written:

> When an essentially cerebral emphasis preponderates in the schooling of the young, as it does in our highly literate society, an alarming incidence of serious failure is to be expected in the difficult passage. `Primitive-Mythology:112`

She is his Age of Anxiety case, product of a "literarily instead of mythologically and ritually educated civilization." And the project itself, a year-12 reading-level text with citations, meant to ground future machines, is the cerebral emphasis at maximum. "Defined, it may be talked about and taught; but talk and teaching cannot produce it."

**Ally here.** On the fourth free thing (the funeral script) Campbell supports the book: rites cluster "at moments of psychological danger," birth, initiation, marriage, death `Primitive-Mythology:455`.

## Challenge 4: Perennialism: Campbell is the "deep betrayal" in person

Part 8 names perennialism the deep betrayal and cites Katz against it. Campbell is the century's most read perennialist: the "unity of the race of man, not only in its biology but also in its spiritual history, which has everywhere unfolded in the manner of a single symphony" `Primitive-Mythology:0`; "all have been built from one fund of mythological motifs" `Primitive-Mythology:5`; Bastian's elementary ideas (*mārga*) against ethnic ones (*deśi*); "Truth is one, the sages call it by many names" `Creative-Mythology:797`. His prescription for the modern individual is to make "intelligent 'making use' not of one mythology only but of all of the dead and set-fast symbologies of the past" `Creative-Mythology:885`, which is the spiritual supermarket the book warns against, endorsed as method.

Two consequences. First, the book's retrieval worry (the machine "means away" minority dharmas by averaging) is not a problem for Campbell; averaging is how you find the elementary ideas. Second, and sharper, Campbell exposes an inconsistency the book has not addressed: it denies perennialism of content while asserting perennialism of function. "One job, many tools," *Ubuntu* and *ma'at* and *Tao* as "sibling names for sibling instances," every society growing one because every society faces one problem: that is a perennialism, at the functional level. Campbell would ask why the book is entitled to its universal and Katz's constructivism at once.

## Challenge 5: The solitary hero against the "we"

The book's hardest practical verdict is that the seeker on the cushion "lacks not sincerity but company," that a personal dharma "needs company, and someone to answer to," and that Sheilaism is one lonely year away. Campbell sides with the seeker.

> The mythogenetic zone is the individual heart... the individual is thrown, willy-nilly, back upon himself... his forest adventurous without way or path. `Creative-Mythology:885`

Parzival's boon is "secular spirituality, sustained by self-responsible individuals acting not in terms of general laws... but each in terms of his own developing realization of worth" `Creative-Mythology:613`. What the book calls Sheilaism, Campbell calls the Grail quest.

Structurally, Campbell's pluralism is a plurality of individuals ("a galaxy of mythologies, as many as the multitude of its geniuses") and his universalism is mankind ("the new focal center of belief and trust is mankind"; national, religious and class mythologies "out of date"). He hollows out the meso-level, the community and the tradition, which is exactly where the book's definition lives.

**Book's position.** The counters are real: the Sosis-Bressler commune data, the loneliness figures, and the "can it bury?" test. Campbell's hero has no funeral rite and no one to wash the body.

**Ally here.** On the procedural test Campbell is close kin. "Individualism and spontaneous pluralism, the free association of men and women of like spirit, under protection of a secular, rational state with no pretensions to divinity, are in the modern world the only honest possibilities: each the creative center of authority for himself" `Creative-Mythology:885`. That is Bakunin's bootmaker with a Grail on top. Living myth works only for those who receive it "uncoerced." The appendix's keystone, revisable by the bound, would get his signature; it is the substantive apparatus (community, floor, construction) he would strike out.

## Challenge 6: Campbell's own reading of "dharma" makes the phrase an oxymoron

Campbell defines dharma as the Orient's answer to the infantile "I want": "a 'thou shalt,' socially applied (not individually determined), which is supposed to be as much a part of the immutable cosmic order as the course of the sun itself," impressed by caste `Oriental-Mythology:22`. In the Orient "there is no idea that men can *decide* what kind of world they want and then bring it to pass" `Oriental-Mythology:618`; *svadharma* is caste duty, not "a vocation elected and realized," which "is distinctly, and uniquely, European" `Creative-Mythology:613`. On his map, dharma is submission to impersonal law and the secular is its opposite; "secular dharma" pairs the two poles.

**Book's position.** The weakest of his challenges, and the book already holds the answers: Part 1's placeholder rule, Olivelle's history of the word as repeatedly re-founded, and above all the Samin. Samin Surosentiko *made* a dharma; the *wong sikep* revised it from inside; Sumarah practises without creed. Campbell's Orient has no such people in it. His Orient/Occident split is the essentialism Part 6 and note 7.5 already anatomise via Said, and his line that the Marxist "irreversible law of history" is *dharma* and *Tao* "applied to the order of man on earth" `Oriental-Mythology:618` dates the analysis to 1962. For the corpus bias flag: the knowledgebase now holds a strong witness that reads dharma as caste-bound "thou shalt" and the individual as a Western invention.

## What this means for the project

Three places the research notes could carry this:

- **1.3 boundary-definition.** Whether "ultimate concern" or the numinous belongs in the feature list, or in the fine print explaining why it was left out. Campbell is the sharpest source for the case that a functionalist definition of dharma is Frazer's mistake.
- **6.1 secular-translation and 8.3 post-traditional.** Campbell's found/made distinction as the mechanism behind the community-deficit concession, and his reversed order (experience, symbol, uncoerced reception) as a rival to the extraction/reconstruction rule.
- **Corpus bias flags.** Campbell adds a perennialist, Jungian, Orient/Occident-essentialist voice to a corpus that leaned constructivist. Flag him as an object of study on the perennialism axis, and turn his functional-universalism point back on the book's own "one job, many tools."
