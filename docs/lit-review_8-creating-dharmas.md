**Date:** 2026-07-28 · **Scope:** downloadable full-text sources bearing on `8-creating-dharmas.md`
and its underlying notes (8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4, 8.5, 7.4, 7.5).

## Why this review exists

Part 8 is the most *contemporary* essay in the series, and that cuts both ways. Where Part 1
leaned on tertiary encyclopedia summaries, Part 8 leans on **corpus podcast transcripts,
popular-synthesis trade books, and named-but-unread primary literature**. The alignment
material is the richest open-access seam in the whole project — almost everything the essay
asserts about machine ethics now has a peer-reviewed or arXiv OA anchor — while the 'Ata
material is the thinnest, and carries a published objection the essay and note 8.5 do not
acknowledge at all.

## Tier 0 — The five that change the most, soonest

1. **The 'Ata retelling debate — Meleika Gesa-Fatafehi, "The real Tongan boys of 'Ata were not
   the real Lord of the Flies", *The Spinoff* (2020; reissued 8 Jan 2021).**
   <https://thespinoff.co.nz/atea/08-01-2021/summer-reissue-the-real-tongan-boys-of-ata-were-not-the-real-lord-of-the-flies>
   `verified`.
   A Tongan and Torres Strait Islander writer names all six men — **Tevita Siola'a, Sione
   Fataua, Luke Veikoso, Fatai Latu, Kolo Fekitoa and Sione "Mano" Totau** — and argues the
   Bregman retelling erases them: it centres the white rescuer, treats *anga fakatonga* (the
   Tongan communal ethic that actually did the work) as invisible, and omits that 'Ata was
   uninhabited *because* its people were seized in the 1863 blackbirding raid. Companion
   coverage: [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2020/05/18/wheres-the-pacific-voice-in-the-viral-real-lord-of-the-flies-story/)
   and [RNZ Pacific](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/416819/concerns-over-a-tale-of-shipwrecked-tongans)
   (both `verified`), carrying a castaway's granddaughter's plea — "PLEASE DO NOT SPEAK OR TALK
   ON BEHALF OF THE 6 BOYS if you're not them or their immediate family" — and Samoan
   journalist Tahlea Aualiitia's objection that "a white man owns the world rights to their
   story." **Note 8.5's honesty section currently discusses n = 1, survivorship and Golding-as-
   foil, but not this.** The essay's Part 8 opens on 'Ata and its whole Mirror section is about
   catalogues assembled from one side of a power imbalance; an unacknowledged Pacific objection
   to *this very story* is the sharpest available instance of its own thesis.

2. **Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin & Hal Ashton, "Beyond Preferences in AI
   Alignment", *Philosophical Studies* (2024).** **OA CC-BY**
   <https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11098-024-02249-w.pdf> `verified`.
   The published philosophical demolition of single-objective, "preferentist" alignment: that
   preferences cannot carry "the thick semantic content of human values", that utility
   representations "neglect the possible incommensurability of those values", and that the fix
   is role-appropriate normative standards "negotiated and agreed upon by all relevant
   stakeholders", yielding "a multiplicity of AI systems … despite our plural and divergent
   values." This is the essay's *entire* flattening schema, argued in a top philosophy journal.
   Citation trajectory 11 (2025) → 16 (2026): rising fast.

3. **Taylor Sorensen et al., "A Roadmap to Pluralistic Alignment" (arXiv 2402.05070, 2024), with
   "Value Kaleidoscope: Engaging AI with Pluralistic Human Values, Rights, and Duties" (*AAAI*
   38, 2024).** **OA**
   <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05070> · <https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/29970/31699>
   both `verified`.
   Kaleido states the mechanism in one sentence — "**As statistical learners, AI systems fit to
   averages by default, washing out these potentially irreducible value conflicts**" — which is
   note 8.4's distributional-flattening thesis, in print, from the field itself. The Roadmap
   adds the *empirical* claim the essay most needs and does not have: "standard alignment
   procedures might **reduce** distributional pluralism in models." It also supplies a working
   taxonomy (Overton / steerable / distributional pluralism) that would sharpen the essay's
   loose "plural but not anything-goes". See also **Feng et al., "Modular Pluralism" (EMNLP
   2024)** <https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.240.pdf> `verified` — LLMs "learn an
   averaged human preference" and can be patched with community models.

4. **Kush R. Varshney, "Decolonial AI Alignment: Openness, Viśeṣa-Dharma, and Including Excluded
   Knowledges", *AAAI/ACM AIES* 7 (2024).** **OA**
   <https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/download/31739/33906> `verified`.
   A senior IBM researcher argues that current alignment practice recapitulates colonial **moral
   absolutism**, and proposes as the remedy *viśeṣa-dharma* — "particular context-specific
   notions of right and wrong" — drawn from Hindu argumentative philosophy, complete with a
   reference architecture. This single paper joins Part 8's two loose ends: it is a published
   ally for the anti-flattening argument **and** it puts *dharma itself* into the alignment
   literature, which is the exact move note 7.5 warns the project about. It cuts both ways, and
   the essay should say so.

5. **Anthropic, *Claude's Constitution* (21 January 2026, ~84 pp., CC0), with "Constitutional AI:
   Harmlessness from AI Feedback" (arXiv 2212.08073) and "Collective Constitutional AI"
   (arXiv 2406.07814 / ACM FAccT 2024).** **OA**
   <https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/d0636f72a9493d279ed36b33987da3430bcb5911/claudes-constitution_webPDF_26-02.02a.pdf>
   · <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08073> · <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07814> — all `verified`
   (announcement: <https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-new-constitution>).
   The essay's opening scene is a *2023* experiment; the constitution it describes has since been
   replaced by a public, freely licensed 84-page document with a four-tier priority hierarchy
   (safety → ethics → compliance → helpfulness). The essay's claim that "its values sit in a
   document anyone can read, argue with, and rewrite" is now literally checkable, and the CCAI
   numbers it quotes (≈1,000 adults, 1,127 statements, 38,252 votes, capability parity, lower
   bias) can be verified against the paper rather than a press summary.

### ▲ Substantive challenges found here
1. **The consent and authorship objection is unanswered in the notes.** Note 8.5's bias section
   flags Bregman's optimism and the corpus's hopeful lean; it does not flag that Tongan writers
   publicly objected to the retelling on grounds of erasure, consent and ownership. Part 8's own
   Mirror section says the answer to Said is "keep the provenance, keep the dissent, keep the
   many voices audibly many" — and here is dissent, from the people the story is about, absent.
2. **"Borrowed" vs. the 1966 headline.** Contemporary reporting says the boys were gaoled on
   return for taking the boat. Nothing in the essay's argument needs the softer verb.
3. **The 1966 film is a re-enactment.** Any use of it must say so.

## 2 · Planetary and climate ethics  (note 8.1)

### The instrument panel, and its critics
- **Richardson, Steffen, Lucht, Rockström et al., "Earth beyond six of nine planetary
  boundaries", *Science Advances* 9 (2023).** **OA**
  <https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adh2458> `browser` (science.org 403s scripted requests;
  opens in one click). 2,931 citations, still climbing steeply (935 → 1,218 → 662 part-year).
  The actual source of note 8.1's [S9] figure — and it says **six**, with ocean acidification
  "close to being breached." The essay's "seven … ocean acidification the latest to fall" comes
  from a later Planetary Health Check; **the two documents must be cited separately and the note
  updated, or the essay and the note will contradict each other in print.**
- **Frank Biermann & Rakhyun E. Kim, "The Boundaries of the Planetary Boundary Framework: A
  Critical Appraisal", *Annual Review of Environment and Resources* 45 (2020).** **OA**
  <https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-environ-012320-080337> `browser`.
  **The counterweight note 8.1 lacks.** Reviews the criticisms from earth-system science,
  development studies and STS — including that the framework's global thresholds are
  scientifically contestable and politically loaded. The note treats the boundaries as a settled
  "instrument panel"; this shows the panel is itself argued over.

### Climate ethics proper — the philosophy the note names but does not read
- **Stephen M. Gardiner, "A Perfect Moral Storm: Climate Change, Intergenerational Ethics and the
  Problem of Moral Corruption", *Environmental Values* 15 (2006)** — `walled` (Sage/JSTOR;
  Unpaywall confirms no OA copy). The book-length version is `lending`:
  <https://archive.org/details/perfectmoralstor0000gard>.
  Gardiner's "moral corruption" is the precise mechanism behind the essay's unexamined move from
  *is* (boundaries crossed) to *ought* (obligation to stay inside): he explains why the present
  generation is *structurally tempted* to defer, which is more than the note's flat is/ought
  observation.
- **Dale Jamieson, *Reason in a Dark Time* (OUP 2014)** — `lending`
  <https://archive.org/details/reasonindarktime0000jami>. The standing argument that the
  *failure* was not of will but of moral equipment — evolved responsibility-attribution cannot
  grip diffuse, deferred, collective harm. Directly supports the essay's "tribal engine"
  paragraph, which currently rests on a prosocial.world blog post [8.1 S1].
- **Bruno Latour, *Facing Gaia*** — `lending`
  <https://archive.org/details/facinggaiaeightl0000lato>. Optional; the note does not need it.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**Whyte's "Our Ancestors' Dystopia Now" is a direct objection to the essay's framing.** Part 8
says conscious dharma-building "falls due" now, because a planet in crisis bears down on us for
the first time. Whyte's position is that for Indigenous peoples the world already ended — the
ecological collapse, the forced relocation, the loss of the relational order — and that treating
the present as an unprecedented emergency is itself a settler perspective. The essay's Mirror
section is about exactly this kind of blind spot and does not catch this one.

### ▲ Substantive challenge found here
**Millière's "shallow alignment" (§3) is the strongest published objection to the essay's own
proposed cure.** The essay ends by recommending that we hand machines "the plurality, the
sources, the disagreements, above all the *reasoning*". Millière's evidence is that current
systems have no robust capacity to *reason over* conflicting norms — they have surface
dispositions that can be played off against one another. Preserving the argument is the right
target; it is not yet something a model can be given.
