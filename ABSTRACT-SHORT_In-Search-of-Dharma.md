# ABSTRACT -- *In Search of Dharma*

Biksu Okusi · Preface, eight essays and a coda · c. 47,000 words · Bali, 2026

A dharma is not a revelation but a technology: an evolved cultural technology worked out by a social animal to solve a permanent problem. Beneath three thousand years of religious accretion lies the Sanskrit root *√dhṛ* -- to hold, support, sustain. *Dharma* is a literal load-bearing word, and "what is a dharma?" is a question about function, not faith: what holds a human life, or a people, together?

Two commitments follow. There is no single *The* Dharma, only dharmas -- secular codes, unwritten peasant ethics, professional creeds and the great religions treated as equally real candidates, none ranked in advance. And the four-feature definition offered here is a disclosed tool, not a recovered essence; Wittgenstein, Gallie and Talal Asad are met head-on, with authorship claimed and innocence disclaimed.

The argument then runs: why every society grows a dharma (the cooperation problem, and what the Axial codes added to a morality already a hundred thousand years old); one hand-made dharma watched close up, the Sedulur Sikep of Java, from a colonial forest road to nine women with their feet in cement at the Presidential Palace; five dharmas that disagree all the way down and rhyme underneath; how a made thing stops feeling made, installing itself beneath the reach of reason; why every failure is the dark twin of a strength, so that no dharma is safe; what survives when grounding, installation and a cooperating world are all lost; and, finally, conscious construction -- for a planet and for machines -- against its besetting danger, the flattening of the plural into one official version.

Evidence was gathered before a word was written, every claim traced to a named source. The criterion the book arrives at is the root word cashed out: a dharma is judged by whether it **holds without crushing** -- a test the book applies, unsparingly, to its own hero case and to itself.
