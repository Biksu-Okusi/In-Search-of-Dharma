# ABSTRACT -- *In Search of Dharma*

Biksu Okusi · Preface + eight parts + coda + appendix · c. 56,000 words · Bali, 2026

## The book in one sentence

A dharma is not a revelation but a technology, worked out by a social animal to solve a permanent problem -- and since dharmas are made rather than found, the question now falling due is whether we can build them consciously without crushing the plurality that makes them work.

## The argument

The book takes a word almost no one can define and almost everyone uses, and follows it to a plain root: Sanskrit *√dhṛ*, to hold, support, sustain -- cognate with Latin *firmus*. Reframed as function rather than faith, "what is a dharma?" becomes "what holds a human life, or a people, together?"

**Part 1** sets two commitments. There is no single *The* Dharma, only dharmas, so secular philosophies, peasant codes, professional ethics and monotheisms all count, none ranked in advance. The definition offered is a disclosed tool, not a recovered essence: Wittgenstein, Gallie and Asad met head-on, innocence disclaimed.

**Part 2** explains why dharmas are universal. Morality is a hundred thousand years older than the codes -- foraging bands ran a demanding order on reputation, story and Boehm's "reverse dominance hierarchy" -- and what broke was scale: above Dunbar's ceiling gossip cannot reach a stranger, and the codes that answered bind by costly commitment. The Axial eruption of 800--200 BCE answers converging pressures, its Eurocentrism flagged.

**Part 3** takes one case with the lid off: the Sedulur Sikep (Samin) of Java, a dharma assembled from about 1890 by an illiterate farmer, still lived -- from deadpan refusal on a colonial forest road to nine women with their feet in wet cement at the Presidential Palace, 2017. Neither world religion nor godless philosophy.

**Part 4** widens to five dharmas that disagree all the way down -- Ubuntu, Stoicism, the Dreaming, Sumarah, the Abrahamic covenant -- the differences real and the family resemblance equally real. Buddhism, the author's home water, goes in the witness chair; the Abrahamic traditions come last, testing whether the non-privileging eye turns on the reader's own.

**Part 5** answers why a made thing stops feeling made. A dharma installs below the reasoning mind -- in moral intuition (Haidt's elephant, WEIRD caveat and all), in habituated practice and ritual, and in the brain itself, the neuroscience deflated where oversold. The machinery is morally blank: it grooves cruelty as faithfully as kindness, labelling both "obvious truth".

**Part 6** turns on the wreckage, refusing the No True Scotsman exit. Every failure is the dark twin of a strength -- the bond is the fence, the path becomes the cage, the blueprint becomes the bulldozer (Year Zero) -- except the insincere predator, for whom a trust-manufacturing dharma is perfect hunting. The author's own Communist Party of Australia years enter as counter-case: a constructed dharma that kept the power to unbuild itself.

**Part 7** asks what survives the loss of grounding, installation and a cooperating world, conceding to Taylor and Asad that "the secular" is a construction site, not a void. No ethic reaches bedrock, only well enough to live by; installation sometimes survives translation (Stoicism-to-CBT) and sometimes hollows out (McMindfulness); and Sumarah is the near-proof -- practice without priesthood, conviction without creed -- yet no tradition has removed its own God from the inside.

**Part 8** takes up conscious construction at two scales: a planetary ethic -- a thin overlapping floor beneath thick plurality, against China's authoritarian singular version -- and the ethics poured into machines. Its central danger has a name, **the flattening**; the book turns that charge on its own RAG corpus, a majority-seeking instrument that already caused one documented error. The answer to relativism is that floor: Curry's cooperative rules, Nussbaum and Sen's capabilities.

**Part 9** states in miniature what the parts establish, and an **appendix**, *Dharmas: The Better Ones*, promotes the verdict to a standard. Two tests: substantive, a dharma **holds without crushing**; procedural and keystone, dharmas are better to the extent they remain revisable by the people bound by them. The first asks what a dharma does, the second who holds the pen; the book's own hero case does not pass cleanly.

## Method

Two separated stages: sourced research notes first, essays written only from those notes -- the reverse of deciding the argument then hunting a quotation. Sourcing is modelled on *isnad*, the chain of transmission. AI did retrieval, note-assembly and citation-checking; judgement and prose are the author's. Notes published openly.

## Stance

Secular, evolutionary, broadly Western and academically inflected -- a partial lens, not a neutral instrument. For an intelligent general reader: no lineage, no authority claimed, no practice being sold.
